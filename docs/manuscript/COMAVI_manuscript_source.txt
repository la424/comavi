# Abstract

%from: p2,p3,p4,p5
Pathogenicity predictors rank missense variants by how likely they are to cause disease, but rarely name the structural process a variant disrupts or the experiment that would test it. COMAVI (COMplex-Aware Variant Impact scoring) uses FoldX to score each substitution twice — in the isolated subunit, one protein chain on its own, and in the assembled protein complex — giving three predicted energy changes: stability in each context and binding to each partner. With a structural-context tier summarizing the substituted residue's surroundings, these give two outputs: a priority score that ranks variants for structural follow-up, and a mechanism profile that names the disrupted processes and the experiments that would test them. We evaluated both on a literature-curated benchmark of 56 variants in 14 protein systems, 13 of them multi-chain complexes, on experimental or AlphaFold 3 structures, in which every expected effect carries a written evidence basis and a citation. Scoring the assembled complex made most disruptions visible: the isolated subunit alone detected 7 of the 29 variants with a committed structural mechanism, the three axes together detected 20, costing three further calls among 27 non-disruptive variants. The mechanism-pattern score was 0.661 against 0.482 for the best structure-blind strategy, and exceeded it at every prespecified threshold. For 18 of the 20 detected mechanisms the expected axis reached the threshold; the main limitation is 9 missed mechanisms, 4 with measured effects below the 2.5 kcal/mol threshold. Against direct measurements the predicted energies ordered the effects correctly but underestimated their magnitude, and the priority score put 17 of the 21 structural mechanisms in the top 20 of 46. The priority score ranks variants for follow-up, and the mechanism profile indicates a stability, assembly or binding experiment. A silent result means the supplied model shows no disruption, not that the variant is benign.

# Author summary

%from: p7,p8,p9
Genetic testing finds far more changes to single protein building blocks than any laboratory can study. Computer tools estimate which changes cause disease but rarely say how, which is what decides the next experiment. Most proteins work while bound to partners. A genetic change can make the protein itself unstable, or leave it folded but weaken its grip on a partner, and the two failures call for different experiments. Our method scores each change twice, in the protein alone and in the assembled group, to tell them apart. We tested it on 56 changes in 14 protein systems whose effects had been established by published experiments. Judging the protein alone found damage in 7 of 29 disruptive changes; judging the assembled group found 20. Its predictions fully matched the published evidence for 35 of the 56 changes and partly for 4 more, and its ranking placed 17 of 21 truly disruptive changes in the top 20 of 46. When it found damage, it named the right problem in 18 of 20 cases; its main weakness was missing 9 disruptive changes, four with only small measured effects. We describe how to choose experiments from its output and when to distrust it.

# Introduction

%from: p11
Sequencing now identifies far more missense variants than can be studied experimentally. Variant effect predictors such as AlphaMissense and GPN-Star rank these variants by predicted pathogenicity or functional constraint [@cheng2023; @ye2026]. AlphaMissense reads sequence, evolutionary signal and structural context, but that context is a predicted single chain, so its view of a residue is the view from one subunit. A pathogenicity score does not say how a variant acts. Similar scores can describe a variant that destabilizes a folded domain, one that weakens an interface and one that alters catalysis, and each calls for a different experiment.

%from: p12
Many proteins function in complexes, and assembly changes the structural context of a residue. A site exposed in an isolated chain may be buried once partners bind. A substitution tolerated by the isolated subunit may destabilize the assembly or weaken one interaction, and an interface substitution can change affinity while leaving the fold intact. Experiments that measure folding and binding separately find distinct energetic effects [@faure2022]. A method meant to guide follow-up should therefore keep the isolated and assembled contexts apart instead of merging them into one impact score. AlphaFold 3 and the Boltz models can now predict complex structures at scale, which extends structural evaluation of variants to complexes that have not been solved experimentally [@abramson2024; @wohlwend2024; @passaro2025].

%from: p15
Several tools place variants on experimental or predicted structures [@arnaudi2025; @khanna2021; @woodard2021; @philipp2024; @moth2026; @kwon2024; @stephenson2024], and two also use the assembled complex. ELASPIC assigns each substitution to a domain core or to an interface and predicts the corresponding change, in folding or in binding energy [@witvliet2016]. MAVISp estimates folding stability on a structure or conformational ensemble of the target protein and, in a separate module, binding energy changes for sites within 10 Å of an interface [@arnaudi2026]. Neither reports the stability of the same substitution both in the isolated subunit and in the assembled complex. That comparison, alongside the change in binding, is what COMAVI adds, and each of its outcomes points to a different experiment. A substitution that destabilizes the isolated subunit predicts a protein that folds poorly and is depleted, which calls for a stability or turnover assay. One that leaves the subunit intact but weakens an interaction predicts a protein that is present but fails to engage its partner, which calls for an affinity measurement against that partner. One that destabilizes only the assembled complex predicts a subunit that folds on its own but forms a less stable complex, which calls for a measurement on the assembled species. A single energy cannot separate these cases, because a residue buried in an interface can destabilize the fold without weakening binding. Stability and interface predictors have been benchmarked against measured energies and deep mutational scans [@buss2018; @rodrigues2019; @montanucci2019; @livesey2020; @blaabjerg2023; @sora2023; @degn2025; @vliora2026], but such benchmarks score energies, not the identification of the disrupted process.

%from: p13
Here we describe COMAVI (COMplex-Aware Variant Impact scoring), a pipeline that scores each substitution in both contexts and keeps its fold and binding predictions separate (Fig 1). COMAVI returns two linked outputs: a priority score, which ranks variants for structural follow-up, and a mechanism profile, which names the process predicted to be disrupted. To test both, we curated a benchmark of 56 variants in 14 protein systems in which every expected effect is tied to a written evidence basis and a citation. We asked five questions of it. What does the assembled complex add over the isolated subunit alone? Does the mechanism profile recover the literature-defined mechanism, and how far does that answer depend on the energy threshold that turns a continuous prediction into a call? How do the predicted energies compare with direct measurements? Does the threshold-free priority score concentrate structural mechanisms at the top of a ranking? Finally, do structural disruption and pathogenicity coincide on the same variants?

@figure Fig 1 | COMAVI workflow. | A missense variant combined with isolated and assembled structural models yields four lines of structural evidence: predicted stability changes in the isolated subunit and in the assembled complex, the binding-energy change for each partner, and the structural-context tier. These combine into two outputs. The priority score ranks the strength of modeled structural disruption and is used to choose variants for further study. The mechanism profile assigns each variant its predicted mechanisms and context, and indicates whether to test stability, assembly or binding. Neither output takes a clinical classification or any other pathogenicity evidence as input; both are computed from structure alone.

# Results

## Every expectation in the benchmark carries its evidence

%from: p22,p27
The benchmark contains 56 missense variants in 14 protein systems (Table 1): 46 variants in 13 multi-chain complexes and 10 in the tandem BRCA1 C-terminal (BRCT) domain, a single-chain system with measured unfolding free energies [@rowling2010]. We chose systems in which published experiments establish how each variant acts, and all energies throughout are FoldX 5.1 predictions (Methods). Together they span the mechanisms COMAVI is meant to separate: loss and gain of function, fold-driven and binding-driven disruption, and variants whose published mechanism does not resolve to a single axis. Each variant is scored on three axes, meaning three independent predicted energy changes: stability of the isolated subunit, meaning the one protein chain taken out of its complex rather than a single residue; stability of that same chain within the assembled complex; and binding energy to each partner. For each variant, the primary literature defines the expected direction on each applicable axis [@clark2022; @starita2015; @brzovic1998; @roehm1997; @crotti2013; @nyegaard2012; @hunter2015; @kosinski2010; @hinrichsen2013; @raevaara2005; @drost2019; @thompson2014; @larsenledet2025; @ollila2006; @ollila2008; @jia2021; @zhao2008; @mandelker2009; @jaiswal2009; @debosscher2004; @legoff2012; @lindsay2025; @lanauze2021; @elliott2000; @frazier2008; @takahashi2001; @stebbins1999; @ohh2000; @pechtl2011; @tischer2025; @eaton1990; @kiger1998; @kwiatkowski1998; @bonaventura1968; @karczewski2020; @landrum2018]. Each of the 97 committed expectations is recorded with its evidence class, a one-line basis and a citation (S1 Table).

Mechanisms counts the variants with a committed structural mechanism; the others are non-disruptive. Pathogenic counts the variants classified as pathogenic or likely pathogenic, by a ClinVar expert panel where one has reviewed the variant [@landrum2018] and otherwise by Franklin [@franklin]. %from: p22,p23
Every benchmark variant is graded. Of the 56, 29 carry a committed structural mechanism and 27 are curated to carry no disruption on the modeled axes, which we call non-disruptive variants. The 46 variants in complexes (21 structural mechanisms and 25 non-disruptive variants) also carry a structural-context tier and form the prioritization population. The 10 BRCT variants (8 fold mechanisms and 2 non-disruptive variants) carry none, because a single chain has no partner. The pathogenicity comparison uses the 39 variants of the prioritization population with a pathogenic or benign clinical classification; the other 7 carry an uncertain-significance classification or none (Fig 2A). Counts below refer to these populations by name.

%from: p28
The 56 variants carry 97 axis expectations, one for each axis on which the literature commits a direction (S1 Table). Forty rest on quantitative measurements (29 energetic, class E1, and 11 functional, E2; Methods), 30 on qualitative experiments (E3), 9 on population frequency (E4) and 18 on inference without an axis-specific assay (E5). Evidence quality differs by direction, because a disruptive effect is far more often followed up with a dedicated biophysical measurement than a normal one is. Thus 28 of the 36 destabilizing expectations are quantitative, whereas all 27 population and inferred expectations are neutral and 22 of them belong to non-disruptive variants. We accepted these weaker classes for neutral expectations because direct measurements showing that a substitution leaves stability and binding unchanged are scarce; without them the benchmark would contain few non-disruptive variants. A non-disruptive commitment therefore records that no axis-specific assay in the primary literature supports a disruption on that axis. It does not record that a structural effect was measured and excluded. Four non-disruptive variants carry no axis expectation at all: CALM1 D96V, N98S and F142L, which act through altered calcium binding [@crotti2013; @nyegaard2012], and SMAD4 I500T, which acts through impaired monoubiquitination [@legoff2012]. They are graded on the whole-variant expectation that no modeled axis is disrupted. How far the results depend on the weaker evidence classes is reported with the mechanism-pattern score below.

## Scoring the assembled complex is what makes most disruptions visible

%from: new (author decision v7.15)
COMAVI's premise is that one calculation on the isolated subunit is not enough, so we first measured what the assembled complex adds on the same variants at the same threshold (Fig 2B). At the 2.5 kcal/mol reference, the isolated-subunit axis alone reached the threshold for 7 of the 29 structural mechanisms and the three axes together for 20, at a cost of three further false calls among the 27 non-disruptive variants. Resampling whole systems puts a 95% interval of 0.18 to 0.82 on the gain in detection rate, and of 0.05 to 0.71 once the added false calls are subtracted. The 13 mechanisms found only through the assembled axes are 6 of the 11 interface destabilizers, 5 of the 6 mixed mechanisms and two of the 11 fold mechanisms, drawn from 7 of the 13 complexes rather than one or two. At every threshold the assembled axes find mechanisms that the isolated subunit misses, from 12 of 24 detections at 1.0 kcal/mol to 13 of 18 at the prediction-interval limit, and they account for every additional false call, a cost that falls from 5 variants at 1.0 kcal/mol to 2 at the limit (Fig 2C; S3 Table, threshold_sweep sheet).

%from: new (v11; axis-design comparison, author decision)
Of the two assembled axes, assembled-complex stability is the one that neither ELASPIC nor MAVISp computes. Removing it from the analysis leaves isolated-subunit stability and binding, the two quantities MAVISp's stability and local-interaction modules estimate. Without it, detection falls from 20 to 15 mechanisms and the number placed on the expected axis from 18 to 9, while two false calls are avoided (Fig 2D). Of the five mechanisms detected only through assembled-complex stability, four are placed on the expected axis; the fifth, PIK3CA E542K, is an interface destabilizer that it reports as instability of the assembly rather than as loss of binding.

%from: p66 (worked examples, moved here from the mechanism section)
Two examples show both what the second context adds and the error it introduces. Complement factor H R78G barely changes isolated-subunit stability (0.93 kcal/mol) but is predicted to weaken binding to C3b by 6.12 kcal/mol, consistent with a measured rise in dissociation constant from 10–14 μM to more than 35 μM [@pechtl2011]. Von Willebrand factor R1334Q leaves the isolated A1 domain unchanged (0.01 kcal/mol) but weakens binding to GPIbα by 4.11 kcal/mol; the purified A1 domain is natively folded, binds GPIbα weakly and supports little platelet adhesion [@tischer2025]. A calculation on the isolated subunit alone would call both variants silent. Weakening an interface does lower the energy of the assembled complex, which is why the two assembled axes are computed separately. BuildModel reports the fold energy of the chain within the complex and AnalyseComplex the interaction energy between chains, so a fold change localized to the interface is not read as a binding change. In both, however, the assembled-complex stability axis also reaches the threshold (4.88 and 4.73 kcal/mol) where the literature implies an intact fold, so both are graded partial rather than consistent.

## COMAVI localizes most of the mechanisms it detects

%from: p55,p65,p73
Each graded variant received a three-level grade (Table 3): 1 when COMAVI recovered the expected combination of effects; 0.5 when it recovered part of it, missing one of several expected effects or adding one the literature does not support; and 0 when it recovered none of them or predicted a change in the opposite direction. Table 2 defines this mechanism-pattern score and every other metric reported below. At the 2.5 kcal/mol (10.5 kJ/mol) reference threshold, the mechanism-pattern score was 37.0 of 56 (0.661). To judge this value, we graded structure-blind strategies, which ignore the structure and give every variant the same mechanism label, for each of the 31 labels COMAVI can assign. The strongest, calling no variant disrupted, scores 0.482 by accepting all non-disruptive variants and missing every mechanism; the three labels that name a structural or interface site without an energy change score the same. Calling every variant destabilized on all three axes scores 0.438, and permuting the ground truth gives a mean of 0.362 (standard deviation 0.056; p = 0.0005; S2 Text). COMAVI exceeds the best structure-blind strategy by 0.179. Because that strategy scores exactly the non-disruptive fraction of the cohort, this margin, rather than the raw score, is the quantity that can be compared across cohorts of different composition. The margin is positive at every threshold and with any one system left out (0.109 to 0.217), but resampling whole systems puts a 95% interval of −0.057 to 0.264 on it, and 7% of resamples give a margin of zero or less. Of the 10.0 grade points by which COMAVI exceeds that strategy, the BRCT domain contributes 5.0 and the hemoglobin tetramer 3.5, while three systems reduce it (S1 Text). The benchmark therefore shows the advantage on these 14 systems but cannot establish that it would hold in others.

Values at each energy threshold are plotted in Fig 4 and tabulated in full in S3 Table, operating_point_sweep sheet. The two isolated-subunit rows recount detection and correct rejection using that axis alone, which is what a single-chain analysis of the same variants would report. ROC AUC is the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve.

%from: p73
The pooled score combines two capabilities that behave differently. The grade averaged 0.517 over the 29 structural-mechanism variants and 0.815 over the 27 non-disruptive variants. Asked as binary questions, the same calls give three rates. At least one axis reached the threshold for 20 of the 29 mechanisms (detection, 0.690), and the axis the literature implicates reached it for 18 (attribution, 0.621). No axis reached it for 22 of the 27 non-disruptive variants (correct rejection, 0.815). The graded structural arm lies below binary attribution at every uniform threshold, by 0.069 to 0.103, so partial credit does not inflate it. COMAVI therefore localizes most of what it detects, and its principal limitation is the 9 mechanisms that no axis detects. For four of these the measured effect is itself below the 2.5 kcal/mol threshold (0.93 to 1.57 kcal/mol: KRAS G12D and G12V, which weaken binding to RAF, and BRCA1 R1751Q and L1664P, which destabilize the BRCT fold), so even an exact prediction would not reach it. For two others the measured effect is large but the predicted energy falls short on every axis (hemoglobin N102T, 3.0 kcal/mol measured, and BRCA1 V1736A, 4.2 kcal/mol), and three have no measured energy (S3 Table, missed_mechanisms sheet).

%from: p66,new (interface-class diagnosis)
Localization errors are few. Of the 20 detected mechanisms, two reached the threshold only on an axis other than the one the literature implicates: PIK3CA E542K, an interface destabilizer that COMAVI calls as instability of the assembly, and MLH1 R755W, a fold mechanism that it calls as strengthened binding (discussed with the ranking below). Resampling whole systems puts a 95% interval of 0.00 to 0.19 on this wrong-axis fraction of the 29 mechanisms. Among the 11 interface variants curated as destabilizers, the binding axis reached the threshold for 4 and the assembled-complex stability axis for 7. An interface substitution can genuinely raise the folding energy of the assembled coordinates, so the two assembled axes are not independent, and for six interface expectations of a change the evidence cannot separate them either: they rest only on assays that report how much complex forms, such as co-immunoprecipitation, pull-down or two-hybrid assays, and a change in the amount of complex can come from a change in binding or in the stability of the assembly. These expectations are therefore met by a change in the measured direction on either assembled axis (Methods). Three of the 18 localized mechanisms, BRCA1 V11G, MLH1 L749P and SMAD4 D351H, are credited in this way; without the rule they would count as detected on the wrong axis (S2 Text).

%from: p74,p73
Direction agreement was similar on the three axes: 19 of 26 expectations for isolated-subunit stability, 17 of 22 for assembled-complex stability and 19 of 25 for binding, rates spanning 0.042. The 6 expectations of a change measured only as an amount of complex were scored jointly, and a call in the measured direction on either assembled axis met 4 of them (59 of the 79 axes overall that can be scored, counting axes rather than variants; Methods). The 46 complex variants and the 10 BRCT variants scored similarly (0.652 and 0.700), so neither subset determines the pooled result.

%from: p78
No single protein system carries the result. Resampling whole systems gave a 95% interval of 0.567–0.764 for the mechanism-pattern score, and leaving out one system at a time gave values from 0.642 to 0.686 (Fig 3). In a sensitivity analysis that gives half credit to calls whose asserted changes all fall on axes the literature never tested, the score rises to 0.670 (S2 Text). Without the four whole-variant non-disruptive variants, the score is 33.0 of 52 (0.635), against 0.471 for the strongest structure-blind strategy on the same variants. Without all seven non-disruptive variants that rest only on population, inferred or whole-variant evidence, the margin narrows to 0.112 (95% interval −0.088 to 0.223). On the 24 variants whose expectations are all quantitative, only four of them non-disruptive, calling every variant destabilized on all three axes scores 0.771, above COMAVI's 0.521. Because these subsets differ widely in class balance, S1 Text also reports the balanced score, the mean of the two arms, on which no structure-blind strategy can exceed 0.5: COMAVI scores 0.634 without the weakly evidenced non-disruptive variants and 0.512 on the quantitative subset. Because the 56 variants come from 14 clustered systems, 0.661 measures agreement on this benchmark, not general accuracy.

(B) Score over 2,000 resamples of the 14 systems; dashed lines mark the 95% interval (0.567–0.764). (C) Score with each system left out in turn; the dashed line marks the full-cohort value. %from: new (author decision v7.15)
Performance was lower on predicted than on experimental complexes. Seven systems use an experimental structure and seven an AlphaFold 3 model; the mechanism-pattern score was 20.0 of 28 (0.714) on the experimental systems and 17.0 of 28 (0.607) on the predicted ones, and the prioritization AUC was 0.948 on the 18 tier-carrying experimental variants against 0.778 on the 28 predicted ones. Resampling whole systems within each source puts a 95% interval of −0.099 to 0.278 on the difference of 0.107, so the benchmark does not resolve it. With seven systems on each side, the source of the model also cannot be separated from the difficulty of the systems. The split is not a clean comparison of model quality in any case: isolated-subunit stability is calculated on a single-chain AlphaFold 3 model in 11 of the 14 systems, including systems whose complex is experimental.

%from: p67,p31
A silent result has two meanings that the benchmark keeps apart. For a variant whose mechanism the supplied model can represent, silence on every applicable axis is an informative negative. When the mechanism acts through a state absent from the model, silence cannot test it. HBB E6V is the clearest case: it acts through polymerization of deoxygenated hemoglobin [@eaton1990], so COMAVI silence points to the missing assembly state, not to a benign variant. A model-scope screen separates the two situations (S2 Table, model_scope sheet).

## Stringency trades detection for correct rejection

%from: p92
Every graded result depends on the energy threshold that turns a continuous prediction into a call (Fig 4). Over the prespecified uniform thresholds of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5 kcal/mol and the per-axis prediction-interval limit (Methods), the mechanism-pattern score was 0.536, 0.571, 0.634, 0.661 and 0.643. The 2.5 kcal/mol reference is the highest of the five on this cohort, as it is for the unweighted mean of the two arms. The system-resampling intervals overlap at every threshold, however (Fig 4A), so the benchmark does not establish it as an optimum.

Throughout, red marks quantities measured on the 29 structural-mechanism variants and gray those measured on the 27 non-disruptive variants. (A) Mechanism-pattern score, pooled (gray band, 95% interval from resampling the 14 systems) and for each arm. (B) Detection, attribution and correct rejection for the 29 structural-mechanism and 27 non-disruptive variants. (C) Direction agreement on each axis (26 isolated-subunit, 22 assembled-complex and 25 binding expectations; the 6 expectations of a change measured only as an amount of complex are scored jointly and given in S3 Table, threshold_sweep sheet); open circles mark the best value for each axis. (D) Measured destabilizations of at least 1.0 kcal/mol (n = 42) predicted at or above the threshold with the same sign.

%from: p92
Raising the threshold changed the three binary rates in different ways. Detection fell: at least one axis reached the threshold for 24 of the 29 mechanisms at 1.0 kcal/mol, for 21 at 1.5, 21 at 2.0, 20 at 2.5 and 18 at the prediction-interval limit. Attribution fell less, from 20 to 18, 18, 18 and 16, so the number of mechanisms detected only on another axis shrank from 4 to 2. Correct rejection rose at every step, from 12 of 27 to 16, 20, 22 and 23. Raising the threshold therefore gains correct rejections by missing disruptions, not by placing them on the wrong axis (Fig 4B). The structural arm fell from 0.621 to 0.448 and the non-disruptive arm rose from 0.444 to 0.852, and the two cross between 1.0 and 1.5 kcal/mol (Fig 4A).

%from: p92
The two stability axes prefer stringent thresholds, and binding is indifferent (Fig 4C). Assembled-complex agreement rose from 12 of 22 at 1.0 kcal/mol to 17 at the reference, and isolated-subunit agreement peaked at 2.0 and 2.5 kcal/mol (19 of 26). Binding agreement was 19 of 25 at every threshold. The denominators do not change across thresholds. Per-axis thresholds therefore offer little: holding the two stability axes at the reference and lowering the binding threshold anywhere in the prespecified range leaves attribution at 18 of 29 and interface attribution at 7 of 12, and at 1.0 or 1.5 kcal/mol costs two correct rejections. Each of the 5 interface mechanisms not localized at the reference carries a predicted binding energy below 1.0 kcal/mol, the most permissive threshold in the prespecified range (median 0.06), so no placement of a binding threshold reaches them. For those variants the predicted binding energy is small where the literature reports a changed interaction, which locates the shortfall in the energy prediction rather than in the calling layer.

%from: p98,p83
Measured effects show the same cost of stringency. We counted the 42 comparisons with a measured destabilization of at least 1.0 kcal/mol, 13 from benchmark variants and 29 from external measurements, and asked whether FoldX predicted a destabilization of at least the threshold with the same sign. It did for 27 at 1.0 kcal/mol, 12 at 2.5 kcal/mol and 8 at the prediction-interval limit (Fig 4D). A stringent threshold can therefore miss a real effect even when the measured destabilization is large.

## Predicted energies rank measured effects but compress their size

%from: p100,p101,p77
The thresholds act on predicted free-energy changes (ΔΔG), so we compared predictions with direct measurements wherever they exist (Fig 5). The pooled set contains 63 comparisons from four systems: 48 binding energies for barnase–barstar and TEM1–BLIP from SKEMPI 2.0 [@jankauskaite2019], 10 unfolding free energies for the BRCA1 BRCT domain [@rowling2010] and 5 hemoglobin assembly energies [@kiger1998; @kwiatkowski1998; @bonaventura1968]. Predicted and measured values were rank-correlated (Spearman ρ = 0.47, p = 1.1 × 10{sup|−4}), but the fitted slope was 0.41 (Fig 5A), and the prediction error became more negative as the measured effect grew (ρ = −0.67; Fig 5B). A measured destabilization of 2.5 kcal/mol corresponds to a predicted 1.42 kcal/mol. FoldX therefore orders effects but compresses large ones, which accounts for part of the loss of measured effects at stringent thresholds.

@figure Fig 5 | Predicted energies rank measured effects but compress their magnitude. | (A) Predicted against measured ΔΔG for 63 comparisons from four systems, with the least-squares fit (slope 0.41) and the identity line; open symbols mark the seven barnase Glu73 substitutions. (B) Prediction error (predicted minus measured) against measured ΔΔG. (C) Isolated-subunit stability for the 10 graded BRCA1 BRCT variants against measured unfolding free energy. (D) Binding energy against measured assembly free energy for the five hemoglobin β-chain benchmark variants at the α1β2 interface. Dash-dot lines mark the 2.5 kcal/mol reference; the dashed line marks identity, where a prediction equals its measurement; positive values are destabilizing.

%from: p77,new (Glu73 disclosure, author decision v7.14)
Seven barnase substitutions at Glu73 behave differently from the rest. All are measured as destabilizing (1.45 to 3.01 kcal/mol) and all are predicted as stabilizing (−1.66 to −1.01 kcal/mol), a systematic sign inversion at a single site. Glu73 is barnase's catalytic general base, buried and unusually strained in the wild-type fold; FoldX scores the relief of that strain as a stabilization while the measurements register the loss of the catalytic arrangement, so the sign disagreement is a property of this site rather than of the substitutions. They are included in every primary statistic. Without them the rank correlation rises to 0.61 (n = 56) while the slope barely changes (0.42), so the compression does not depend on this cluster. For the SKEMPI binding comparisons alone, ρ = 0.34 (p = 0.019, n = 48), and the predicted sign was correct for 30 of 40 measured destabilizations. Without Glu73, ρ = 0.55 (p = 0.0002, n = 41), and the sign was correct for 30 of 33 (S1 Fig). At the 2.5 kcal/mol reference, measured and predicted calls agreed for 42 of the 63 comparisons (S2 Fig).

%from: p104,p106
Two axes also have direct anchors within the benchmark's own systems. Predicted isolated-subunit stability tracked measured unfolding free energy across the 10 graded BRCT variants (ρ = 0.72; Fig 5C). The only one measured above 2.5 kcal/mol but predicted below it is V1736A (measured 4.20, predicted 1.24 kcal/mol). Predicted binding tracked measured hemoglobin assembly energies for four of the five variants (ρ = 0.80, n = 5; Fig 5D); the exception is HBB N102T, measured at 3.00 kcal/mol per tetramer and predicted at −0.14 kcal/mol, the sub-threshold interface expectation discussed above. The largest measured penalty, HBB W37E at 9.00 kcal/mol, was predicted at 5.14 kcal/mol, so magnitude is underestimated without changing any call at 2.5 kcal/mol. The benchmark contains no direct measurement of assembled-complex stability, so that axis is tested only against literature-defined directions.

%from: p107
These comparisons support reporting the sign, rank and continuous value of each predicted energy with its replicate spread and threshold. They do not support treating a FoldX ΔΔG as a measured energy.

## The priority score concentrates structural mechanisms at the top of the ranking

%from: p80,p81,p77
The priority score combines a context component and an energy component (Methods), and we first tested each alone on the 46-variant prioritization population. The structural-context tier uses no energy. It placed 17 of the 21 structural-mechanism variants in Tier 1 or 2 (sensitivity 0.810, 95% confidence interval 0.581–0.946) together with 12 of the 25 non-disruptive variants (specificity 0.520, 0.313–0.722; Fisher p = 0.032, within-system permutation p = 0.165). The tier is therefore permissive and, on this cohort, not separated from chance once system membership is held fixed. Because it cannot identify which process is affected, it never overrides an axis energy. Interface status alone was comparably specific (0.720) and recovered 15 of the 21 mechanisms, and the interface bonus is what promotes those 15 (S2 Table, context_comparators sheet; S3 Fig). On the 26 variants whose every committed axis has E1 to E3 support, sensitivity was unchanged at 0.812 and specificity was 0.600 (S1 Text).

%from: p82,p84
We measured ranking quality by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC AUC), the probability that a randomly chosen structural-mechanism variant outranks a randomly chosen non-disruptive variant. The energy component alone reached an AUC of 0.779 and placed 15 of the 21 mechanisms in the top 20, and 16 by rank 25. It ranks HBB N102T 36th and MLH1 Q542L 32nd of 46, although the tier places both in Tier 1. The context component alone reached 0.785 and recovered 11 mechanisms in the top 20 and 13 by rank 25. The two components fail on different variants. Crossing the energy calls with the tier gives four descriptive evidence states, according to whether an axis reaches the threshold and whether the tier is 1–2: convergent, energy only, context only and neither. At 2.5 kcal/mol, 15 mechanisms are convergent, 2 are context-only and 4 fall in the neither state, while the energy-only state holds 3 non-disruptive variants and no mechanism. At 1.0 kcal/mol the energy-only state holds 6 non-disruptive variants and 2 mechanisms (S3 Table, evidence_states sheet). At the reference threshold, an energy call without supporting structural context marks a non-disruptive variant in this cohort, but that separation does not hold at the screening default.

%from: p83,p84,p85
Combining the two components recovered what neither reaches alone (Fig 6). The priority score separated the two classes (Fig 6A) and placed 9 of the 21 structural mechanisms in the top 10 and 17 in the top 20, with no further recovery by rank 25 (Fig 6B). Its ROC AUC was 0.834 and its average precision 0.779, against a positive rate of 21 in 46; without the four whole-variant non-disruptive variants, the AUC was 0.837. The ranking applies no energy threshold, and its positive class comes from the curated mechanism class, so none of these values depends on the threshold.

(B) Structural mechanisms recovered as a function of rank depth for the priority score and for its context and energy components ranked alone.

%from: p89,p90
A high rank does not guarantee a correct mechanism. Of the 17 structural mechanisms in the top 20, 8 are graded consistent, 4 partial and 5 inconsistent. The four outside it, at ranks 32 to 38, are KRAS G12D and G12V, whose measured losses of RAF binding (0.93 and 1.18 kcal/mol) are below the 2.5 kcal/mol reference, and MLH1 H718Y and SMAD4 I500V, whose effects were measured without an energy. Three of the five inconsistent grades are expected losses of binding that COMAVI either placed on another axis (PIK3CA E542K, as instability of the assembly) or called at an interface site with no energy change (MLH1–PMS2 Q542L and hemoglobin N102T). The other two, MLH1 R755W and PIK3CA E545K, fail in a different way. FoldX returns a large favorable interaction energy for each (−9.7 and −4.6 kcal/mol), which COMAVI reads as strengthened binding. For E545K the axis is right but the direction is wrong, because releasing the inhibitory contact with p85 is energetically favorable [@zhao2008]. For R755W the literature reports reduced abundance with binding preserved [@kosinski2010; @larsenledet2025], and neither stability prediction reaches the threshold (0.97 kcal/mol for the isolated subunit and −0.94 in the complex). These two variants would send a user to the right protein with the wrong experiment (S3 Text).

## Structural prioritization and pathogenicity prediction answer different questions

%from: p109,p23
Clinical annotation and modeled structural disruption come apart inside this cohort. Of the 28 variants classified as pathogenic among the 39 in the comparison, 14 are curated to carry no disruption on any modeled axis and 14 carry a structural mechanism. Half of the pathogenic class is therefore invisible to a structural method by construction, which is the strongest statement the benchmark makes about the two properties being distinct.

%from: p109,new (design disclosure, author decision v7.15)
Rank-based comparison with a pathogenicity predictor is correspondingly limited. Ten of the 11 benign variants in this set entered the benchmark as curated non-disruptive controls, so any structural score separates them from pathogenic structural mechanisms partly by construction, and a pathogenicity AUC computed here measures the cohort as much as the method. The exception is informative: MLH1 H718Y is clinically benign and carries a committed monomer-fold destabilization, because the source reports reduced thermal stability and a half-life of 64% of wild type alongside proficient mismatch repair. A variant can be structurally destabilized and clinically neutral at once, which is the separation the two outputs are meant to preserve. With that caveat, AlphaMissense ranked pathogenicity better (ROC AUC 0.873) than the energy component (0.818), the priority score (0.795) or the context component (0.679) (Fig 7). This set is the part of the prioritization population that carries a pathogenic or benign classification, but the positive classes differ, clinical label against curated mechanism, so these values are not comparable with the structural AUC of 0.834.

Filled circles, pathogenic (including gain of function); open circles, benign; gray band, the AlphaMissense ambiguous range (0.34–0.564). Labelled variants are discussed in the text.

%from: p110
Discordant variants show what each view adds. HBB E6V is pathogenic but scores low on both AlphaMissense (0.223) and the priority score (0.275), because its mechanism is absent from the supplied model. CFH R78G has a high priority score (0.818) and a named binding disruption. PIK3CA H1047R sits in Tier 2 context but has no energy call on any axis at the reference threshold, so COMAVI ranks it on context alone and names no mechanism. Both of these clinically pathogenic variants, R78G and H1047R, fall in the AlphaMissense ambiguous range (0.398 and 0.538), as does one benign variant, MSH2 N127S. Where the pathogenicity score is least decided, the structural profile supplies a named mechanism for one variant and a place to look for another.

%from: p113
The two views are therefore asymmetric in use: a structural call supplies a testable hypothesis, though a disrupted structure is not by itself evidence of disease, whereas a silent result constrains nothing about pathogenicity (S3 Text).

# Discussion

## Using the two outputs

%from: p117,p114,p120
COMAVI answers two practical questions: which variants deserve structural follow-up, and which experiment should test the predicted effect. Its two outputs fail differently: the priority score carries no threshold, whereas the mechanism profile depends on the one at which a continuous energy becomes a call. A practical workflow therefore has two steps. The priority score allocates experimental effort; the mechanism profile then names the measurement, because each axis points to the measurement class used to test it in this benchmark. An isolated-subunit call predicts a chain that folds less stably on its own, which an unfolding free energy or thermal stability measurement on the isolated protein tests directly, as for the BRCT domain here. A binding call predicts weaker affinity for a named partner, which a titration against that partner tests, as for barnase–barstar and TEM1–BLIP. A call on assembled-complex stability without a binding call predicts a complex that is less stable once formed rather than harder to form, and those separate in a measurement on the assembled species, as for the hemoglobin tetramer. Where two axes reach the threshold together, the profile names both, and the informative experiment is the one that tells them apart. The signed energies are read with their uncertainty, threshold, tier and model limitations throughout. A high priority score with no energy call is a reason to examine a site, not a named disruption.

%from: p122,p123,p52
The threshold should follow the cost of error. The 1.0 kcal/mol default suits screening, where a missed mechanism costs more than extra follow-up; 2.5 kcal/mol is the benchmark reference; and the prediction-interval limit suits settings where a false mechanistic claim is costly. These thresholds separate curated mechanisms from non-disruptive variants, a different question from the 3 kcal/mol cut-off that MAVISp and related MLH1 studies use to predict cellular degradation [@arnaudi2026; @abildgaard2019; @nielsen2017]. Because each call is reported with its continuous energy, a user can re-read it at another threshold; 14 of the 56 variants change grade across the four uniform thresholds, and 17 across all five operating points (S2 Table, benchmark_variants sheet). A silent result means that the supplied static model shows no disruption at the stated threshold. It does not mean that a variant is benign, nonfunctional or unworthy of study.

%from: p124
COMAVI complements pathogenicity predictors, population data, functional assays and clinical interpretation tools such as Franklin [@franklin]. Shown side by side, a pathogenicity score and the COMAVI outputs disagree in interpretable ways (Fig 7). Combining them into one score would reintroduce the conflation that separating structure from pathogenicity is meant to remove.

## Relation to existing frameworks

%from: p118
MAVISp is the closest existing framework [@arnaudi2026]. Both use FoldX BuildModel for stability and AnalyseComplex for interaction energy, averaged over replicates, and both report fold and binding effects separately; MAVISp also runs a consensus of predictors and covers more than a thousand proteins. COMAVI differs in three ways. First, it scores each substitution in both the isolated subunit and the assembled complex and contrasts the two, whereas MAVISp estimates folding stability on a structure or conformational ensemble of the target protein and, in its local-interaction module, binding free energy for sites within 10 Å of an interface. Second, it evaluates mechanism attribution against literature-defined expectations for each axis and against measured energies. MAVISp is validated against functional assays and clinical labels; its authors set binding-energy thresholds from the expected error of the predictors and note that measured effects on protein–protein interactions are scarce and heterogeneous enough to complicate reliable benchmarking. The two designs trade scale against the availability of a measured answer. Third, COMAVI is a pipeline that runs on a user's own structures and variants, whereas MAVISp is a curated database to which trained biocurators add proteins, including targets that users request.

%from: p119
Two of these differences stand on their own, independently of the pipeline. The first is the benchmark itself: every expectation carries a written basis and a citation, and an automated check enforces their agreement. The second is the comparison of predictions with measured energies rather than with other predictors.

## Limitations and next tests

%from: p126
FoldX preserves the direction and rank of an effect better than its size, here and in the literature. Published correlations with measured stability changes range from 0.19 to 0.81, and the uncertainty of a single prediction is large [@sapozhnikov2023]; evaluations report qualitative agreement more often than quantitative agreement [@buss2018]. Large destabilizations are therefore understated, stringent thresholds miss real effects, and the priority score cannot be read in experimental energy units.

%from: p126
A related caution applies to small effects. A variant below the threshold is reported as carrying no modeled disruption on that axis, not as having no effect. Modest destabilizations can matter where a small change in stability or affinity alters assembly or turnover, and the measured-recovery counts above show how many of them a stringent threshold discards. A study concerned with mild perturbations should therefore use the lower threshold and accept more false calls. Lowering the threshold, however, does not reach every small effect. Ten committed destabilizing expectations fall below even 1.0 kcal/mol, and within that range the predicted magnitude does not distinguish them from those the literature calls intact (AUC 0.65, 95% confidence interval 0.40 to 0.86). A prediction below the lowest threshold is therefore silence on that axis, not weak evidence of disruption.

%from: p127
The benchmark is small and clustered: 56 graded variants in 14 systems, with the variants of a system sharing structures and curation. Resampling whole systems addresses this dependence but cannot replace prospective validation. The same benchmark informed the development of the priority score, so its ranking figures, including the AUC of 0.834, are in-sample estimates; the formula is frozen, which is what makes an out-of-sample test possible, but this benchmark cannot provide one. Performance was also lower on predicted than on experimental complexes, by an amount this cohort cannot resolve. Evidence is uneven as well: isolated-subunit stability and binding have direct energy anchors, assembled-complex stability has none, and four non-disruptive variants are graded only at the whole-variant level.

%from: v11 Limitations; reference_outputs/COMAVI_evidence_sensitivity.json; reference_outputs/COMAVI_detection_vs_attribution.json
The advantage over structure-blind strategies rests largely on non-disruptive variants with weaker evidence and on two systems. On the 24 variants whose every expectation is quantitative, only four of them non-disruptive, calling every variant destabilized on all three axes scores 0.771 against 0.521 for COMAVI, and the balanced score is 0.512 against a ceiling of 0.5 for any structure-blind strategy; of the 10.0 grade points by which COMAVI exceeds calling no variant disrupted, the BRCT domain contributes 5.0 and the hemoglobin tetramer 3.5 (S1 Text). Localization also depends on how the six expectations of a change measured only as an amount of complex are scored: without the evidence-resolution rule, 15 rather than 18 of the 29 mechanisms are localized (S2 Text).

%from: p128
COMAVI evaluates static coordinates. It cannot capture catalytic, dynamic, aggregative, regulatory, post-translational, ligand-dependent or other state-dependent mechanisms, so model limitations must be assessed before a silent result is interpreted.

%from: p129
The most informative next tests are external panels that separate folding from binding and include both disruptive and function-retaining variants. More variants in the VWF A1–GPIbα and complement factor H–C3b systems would strengthen the evidence on fold-neutral binding disruptions, and SOD1, transthyretin and the dense PDZ and SH3 landscapes can test coupled tertiary, quaternary and allosteric effects [@faure2022; @vassall2006; @hurshman2008]. The priority-score formula is frozen and should be evaluated on such panels without retuning. Extending the tier and the priority score to monomer-only systems would require a prespecified formula and prospective validation.

## Conclusion

%from: p131
COMAVI turns a list of missense variants into a fixed priority for structural follow-up and a mechanism profile that proposes the disrupted process and the experiment to test it. On this benchmark it detected most literature-defined mechanisms and localized most of those it detected, ranked structural mechanisms near the top, and failed most often by missing a disruption whose predicted energy stayed below the threshold. Its value lies in naming the next experiment — a stability measurement on the isolated chain, a titration against a named partner, or a measurement on the assembled species — rather than in predicting pathogenicity.

# Materials and methods

## COMAVI outputs

%from: p18
COMAVI takes a missense variant and a structural model of its complex and calculates three FoldX energy changes: the stability of the isolated subunit, the stability calculated on the assembled coordinates and the interaction energy with each partner. A structural-context tier summarizes the local environment of the substituted residue without using energy. From these, COMAVI returns the priority score, which ranks variants by the strength of modeled structural disruption, and the mechanism profile, which keeps the signed energies, the tier, their uncertainty, the threshold and the model limitations. A profile may support an isolated-subunit effect, an assembled-complex effect, a binding effect, more than one of these, or none. The energies, the tier and the priority score are continuous. A threshold is needed only to turn the energies into the discrete calls that are graded against categorical literature expectations, so the threshold belongs to this evaluation, not to the method.

## Structures and energy calculations

%from: p33,p34,p35
COMAVI scores a variant on a supplied model of a complex whose composition is known; it does not discover partners. Seven systems used experimental structures and seven used models from the AlphaFold 3 server (Table 1) [@abramson2024]. The isolated subunit is a separate single-chain model, not a chain extracted from the complex. Eleven of the 14 systems use a single-chain AlphaFold 3 model of the relevant gene, including systems whose complex is experimental; the BRCT, VWF A1 and complement factor H systems use the experimental chain. The two stability axes are therefore calculated on different coordinates. That difference is what makes the contrast between them informative, and it is also a source of error. Energies were calculated with FoldX 5.1 [@schymkowitz2005; @delgado2019] using default energy-function parameters. Energies are reported in kcal/mol, the convention of the stability literature; 1 kcal/mol is 4.184 kJ/mol. Each structure was repaired once with RepairPDB. BuildModel was run five times per variant on the isolated subunit and on the assembled complex, and AnalyseComplex was applied to each mutant complex to obtain the change in interaction energy with each partner. ΔΔG is mutant minus wild type in kcal/mol, so positive values are destabilizing; the axis value is the mean over replicates, and the replicate standard deviation is kept. Summary outputs use the most disruptive partner, and partner-level values remain available. Running BuildModel and AnalyseComplex separately keeps an interface-localized fold signal from being read as a binding change. S1 Text gives the commands.

## Structural confidence

%from: p37
FoldX calculates ΔΔG from the supplied coordinates, and the predicted local distance difference test (pLDDT) of an AlphaFold model does not rescale these energies. Instead pLDDT decides whether a predicted axis is used at all, using the confidence bands of the AlphaFold database [@tunyasuvunakool2021]. Two thresholds act at once rather than defining alternative settings. An axis is admitted when the pLDDT at the variant site reaches 50. The isolated-subunit axis follows the site pLDDT in the single-chain model, and each partner's axes follow the site pLDDT in the complex. An axis below 50 contributes neither a mechanism call nor a term to the priority score, and that partner adds no contacts, interface occupancy or burial to the tier. At 70 the derived confidence of a variant's energies is upgraded from medium to high, which the concordance analyses use. Independently of either, the tier's context score is multiplied by 0.7 when the best site pLDDT lies between 50 and 70 and by 0.4 below 50; experimental structures are not attenuated.

## Structural-context tier and priority score

%from: p39,p40
The structural-context tier describes how strongly a site is embedded in features that make it sensitive to substitution. It contains no energy and is not a mechanism call. It combines substitution severity, scored by the Grantham distance [@grantham1974], with local contacts, interface occupancy, burial and model confidence. The fixed total maps to Tier 1 (strongest context) through Tier 4 (weakest), and S1 Text gives the weights and cut-offs. Because interface occupancy contributes directly to the tier, we compared the full tier with interface status alone and with the tier recalculated without its interface bonus (S2 Text).

%from: p41,p42
The integrated structural-disruption score (ISDS-v1), called the priority score here, combines energy and context with a fixed formula: the same reference values and tier mapping apply to every variant set, and no coefficient is fitted to the cohort. For each confidence-passing energy j, |ΔΔG{sub|j}| is divided by a reference value τ{sub|j} of 2.9 kcal/mol for the two stability axes and 3.5 kcal/mol for binding. These are the upper bounds of the published FoldX 95% prediction intervals [@sapozhnikov2023]. Here they serve as divisors that put the three axes on one scale, not as cut-offs, so an energy below τ{sub|j} still contributes. The largest ratio represents the strongest reliable energetic signal without summing correlated stability effects:

@eq R = \max_{j} \frac{|ΔΔG_{j}|}{τ_{j}}

%from: p44
R is compressed so that a single extreme prediction cannot dominate the ranking:

@eq E = \frac{R}{1 + R}

%from: p46
The tier T is converted to a context component C, which maps Tiers 1 to 4 to 1, 2/3, 1/3 and 0:

@eq C = \frac{4 − T}{3}

%from: p48,p49
The priority score weights the two components equally:

@eq S = \frac{E + C}{2}

%from: p49,p41
The priority score does not change with the threshold used for mechanism calls, and the prioritization results therefore hold at every threshold. It is not a pathogenicity probability, it does not identify the disrupted process, and no binary cut-off on it has been validated. The Tier 1–2 cut-off is used only in the descriptive comparator analyses.

## Energy thresholds and evidence states

%from: p51,p53
A predicted energy becomes a call on an axis when its absolute value reaches the threshold. Five operating points were fixed before scoring: uniform thresholds of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5 kcal/mol, and the prediction-interval limit. The limit takes, separately for each axis, the upper edge of the 95% prediction interval fitted to FoldX folding and binding predictions [@sapozhnikov2023]: 2.9 kcal/mol for isolated-subunit stability, 2.9 for assembled-complex stability and 3.5 for binding. A call that survives that limit is unlikely to arise from prediction error alone. The released software uses 1.0 kcal/mol as a screening default, and 2.5 kcal/mol is the benchmark reference. S3 Table (threshold_sweep sheet) reports post hoc points above that limit. Crossing the energy calls with the tier (Tier 1–2 against Tier 3–4) defines four descriptive evidence states: convergent, energy only, context only and neither (S2 Text).

## Benchmark curation

%from: p27
The primary literature defined the expected direction on each applicable axis, and each expectation was classified by what its source measured. Class E1, quantitative energetic, is a reported thermodynamic or kinetic quantity for the substitution, such as a melting-temperature shift, an unfolding free energy or a dissociation constant. E2, quantitative functional, is a numerical assay readout that stands in for one of these, such as a deep-mutational-scanning abundance or interaction score. E3, qualitative experimental, is a direct observation reported without a number, such as loss of co-immunoprecipitation. E4, population frequency, is inference from allele frequency or constraint without an assay of the variant. E5, inferred, is an expectation implied by an established mechanism without a measurement of that axis; a variant shown to bind normally while losing catalytic activity, for example, implies a neutral binding axis. The absence of an assay was never treated as evidence: without a published result implying the axis state, the axis was left uncommitted. S1 Table gives the class, basis and citation of all 97 committed expectations, and an automated check enforces agreement between each committed direction and its written basis (S3 Text). Expectations were curated from the primary literature; during development, expectations found to contradict their own written basis were corrected, and every correction is recorded with its reason (S3 Text).

%from: new (v11; reviewer comments 3 and 6-10)
Five scored candidates were excluded from the benchmark because no gradeable expectation could be defined for them. For TNNI3 R162W, VHL W117R and MSH2 G674R, the primary literature commits no direction on any modeled axis. BRCA1 R1699L and R1699Q disrupt phosphopeptide recognition, a protein interaction whose partner is absent from the supplied structure. Variants whose mechanism lies outside the processes COMAVI scores, such as calcium binding or monoubiquitination, were instead retained and graded on the expectation that no modeled axis is disrupted. The five are listed with their COMAVI outputs in S2 Table (excluded_variants sheet).

%from: new (v11; clinical labels and AlphaMissense provenance)
Clinical labels enter only the pathogenicity comparison, and each follows one rule. A ClinVar classification reviewed by an expert panel or given in a practice guideline takes precedence [@landrum2018]; otherwise the Franklin classification is used [@franklin]. ClinVar records for the 46 variants in complexes were retrieved through NCBI E-utilities on 10 November 2026 and are distributed with the code. Fourteen variants carry an expert-panel classification: it agrees with Franklin for twelve, changes PMS2 G857A from a Franklin variant of uncertain significance to benign, and for PIK3R1 M326I is split between two records for different nucleotide changes, so Franklin is used. Pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants are pathogenic, benign or likely benign variants are benign, and variants of uncertain significance or without a classification are not compared. AlphaMissense scores were taken from the published release (doi:10.5281/zenodo.8208688) [@cheng2023] at UniProt canonical positions, with the HBB variants converted from mature-chain numbering and the two reverse-named controls described below looked up as their clinical substitutions.

%from: p30,p31,p33
For each variant, we asked whether the supplied structure contains the context its known mechanism needs: the relevant partner, ligand, modification, oligomeric state and conformation. When the context was absent, a silent result could not test the mechanism and was treated as a limitation of the model, not as negative evidence. The isolated BRCT structure, for example, represents fold energetics but not phosphopeptide recognition by R1699L and R1699Q; HBB E6V requires the deoxygenated polymerization state; VHL Y98H lacks the bound hypoxia-inducible factor peptide; and CALM1 N98S depends on calcium-linked dynamics. S2 Table (model_scope sheet) gives the rules.

## Outcomes and statistics

%from: p55,p58
The mechanism-pattern score is the mean of a three-level grade (Table 3): 1 for a fully consistent whole-variant pattern, 0.5 for a pattern that recovers at least one expected effect but misses another or adds an unsupported one, and 0 for a pattern that recovers no expected effect or predicts the opposite direction. It is a weighted mechanism consistency, not exact-match accuracy, and unknown or inapplicable axes are omitted. Direction agreement counts, for each axis, the expectations whose call matches the committed direction, irrespective of the size of the predicted energy. An expectation is scored only when its axis passes the confidence gate and the interval of the predicted energy, the replicate mean ± 1.96 replicate standard deviations, excludes zero, so a prediction indistinguishable from no change counts neither for nor against agreement; 79 of the 97 expectations qualify, and the interval check removes the other 18 (S1 Text). Detection, attribution and correct rejection ask the same question as binary rates (Table 2). Ten binding expectations rest only on assays that report how much complex forms (immunoprecipitation, pull-down, two-hybrid, TR-FRET or multiplexed interaction assays), and the evidence ledger names the assay for each (S1 Table). A change in the amount of complex can come from weaker binding or from a less stable assembled complex, which COMAVI computes separately, so for the six of these that expect a change, a change in the measured direction on either assembled axis meets the expectation; the four that expect no change are scored on binding alone (S2 Text). This holds wherever an expectation is scored: in grading, in attribution, in the sub-threshold analysis and in direction agreement, where the six form a joint row and each axis's own row counts only expectations specific to that axis. The rule is fixed by the evidence before any call is considered and applies equally to every structure-blind strategy; a call in the opposite direction on an assembled axis earns nothing from it. It is not applied in reverse: a normal amount of complex supports the committed binding expectation but cannot show that the assembled complex is stable, so no assembled-complex expectation was added for non-disruptive variants. As a sensitivity analysis only, a call that recovers no expected effect but asserts changes solely on axes the literature never tested, and contradicts nothing measured, was credited 0.5 (S2 Text).

@table table3 | Table 3 | Whole-variant grading rubric. | Each graded variant receives one grade at each threshold; the mechanism-pattern score is the mean grade.

%from: p58,p83
Variants are named as their primary literature names them. For 48 of the 56 that is the numbering of the UniProt reference sequence; the six HBB variants use mature-chain numbering, so HBB E6V is p.Glu7Val. Two benign controls are named in the reverse direction, because the deposited structure each was modeled on carries the substituted residue rather than the reference one. 2WII carries Ile where the reference sequence has Val62, and 1SQ0 carries Ala where it has Thr1381. The calculations labeled CFH I62V and VWF A1381T are therefore the reverse of p.Val62Ile and p.Thr1381Ala. Both are curated non-disruptive controls, and on each of the three axes both predict below 0.72 kcal/mol, under the lowest threshold considered, so the direction of the calculation affects no reported result.

%from: p58,p83
A measured destabilization counts as recovered at a threshold when the predicted energy reaches that threshold with the same sign; the population is every comparison with a measured destabilization of at least 1.0 kcal/mol. Calibration used Spearman correlation and least-squares regression of predicted on measured ΔΔG.

%from: p59
For prioritization, the positive class is the 21 structural-mechanism variants and the negative class the 25 non-disruptive variants in the 46-variant population. Average precision summarizes precision over the recall curve, and top-k recovery counts the structural mechanisms among the k highest-ranked variants. Alternative formulas, score bands and component analyses are internal sensitivity analyses, because the same benchmark informed the development of the score (S2 Text; S4 Fig). Within-system permutation tests for the tier screens used exact convolved hypergeometric distributions.

%from: p60
Variants in a system share structures and curation, so the main uncertainty analysis resampled whole systems (2,000 draws). We also report leave-one-system-out values, variant-level bootstrap intervals, label permutations and replicate noise; S1 Text lists the seeds and draw counts. Confidence intervals for proportions are exact binomial intervals.

Scoring and statistics used Python 3.11 with pandas 2.3.3, NumPy 2.4.6, SciPy 1.17.1 and scikit-learn 1.9.0. Code, reference outputs, figure sources and regression tests are available at https://github.com/la424/comavi. The priority score is implemented once in a shared engine, so benchmark and external runs receive the same versioned fields. FoldX, which is licensed separately, and the large collections of structural inputs are not redistributed.
