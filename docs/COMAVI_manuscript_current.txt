Abstract
Pathogenicity predictors rank missense variants by how likely they are to cause disease, but they rarely name the structural process a variant disrupts or the experiment that would test it. COMAVI (COMplex-Aware Variant Impact scoring) scores each substitution twice, in the isolated subunit and in the assembled complex, and reports three predicted energy changes: isolated-subunit stability, assembled-complex stability and binding to each partner. With a structural-context tier, these yield two outputs: a priority score that ranks variants for structural follow-up, and a mechanism profile that names the disrupted process and therefore the experiment. We evaluated both against a literature-curated benchmark of 61 variants in 14 protein systems, in which every expected effect carries a written evidence basis and a citation. At the 2.5 kcal/mol reference threshold, the mechanism-pattern score was 41.0 of 57 graded variants (0.719), against 0.561 for the strongest trivial strategy. COMAVI detected a lesion in 20 of 25 variants with a structural mechanism but attributed it to the axis the literature implicates in only 14. Among 47 variants with a structural-context tier, the priority score placed 16 of 17 structural mechanisms in the top 20 and all 17 in the top 25. Across the prespecified thresholds of 1.0 to 2.5 kcal/mol, higher stringency left detection nearly unchanged, lowered attribution and raised correct rejection, and the binding axis preferred a lower threshold than the two stability axes. Predicted energies ranked measured effects (Spearman ρ = 0.48, n = 63) but compressed their size (slope 0.42). COMAVI is therefore best used in two steps: the priority score to decide which variants to study, then the mechanism profile to choose a stability, assembly or binding experiment. A silent result means that the supplied model shows no lesion, not that the variant is benign.
Author summary
Genetic testing finds far more changes to single protein building blocks than any laboratory can study. Computer tools estimate which changes are likely to cause disease but rarely say how a change does its damage, which is what decides the next experiment. Most proteins work while bound to partners. A change can make the protein itself unstable, or it can leave the protein folded but weaken its grip on a partner, and the two problems are tested in different ways. Our method evaluates each change twice, in the protein alone and in the assembled group of proteins, so that the two problems can be told apart. We tested it on 61 changes in 14 protein systems whose effects published experiments have already established. Of the 57 changes we could grade, its predictions fully matched the published evidence for 36 and partly matched it for 10 more. In a ranked list of 47 changes, it placed 16 of the 17 truly disruptive ones in the top 20. Its main weakness was placing detected damage in the wrong part of the structure. We describe how to use the method to choose experiments, and when not to trust it.
Introduction
Sequencing now identifies far more missense variants than can be studied experimentally. Variant effect predictors such as AlphaMissense and GPN-Star rank these variants by predicted pathogenicity or functional constraint, using sequence and evolutionary information [1,2]. A pathogenicity score does not say how a variant acts. Similar scores can describe a variant that destabilizes a folded domain, one that weakens an interface and one that alters catalysis, and each calls for a different experiment.
Many proteins function in complexes, and assembly changes the structural context of a residue. A site exposed in an isolated chain may be buried once partners bind. A substitution tolerated by the isolated subunit may destabilize the assembly or weaken one interaction, and an interface substitution can change affinity while leaving the fold intact. Experiments that measure folding and binding separately find distinct energetic effects [3]. A method meant to guide follow-up should therefore keep the isolated and assembled contexts apart instead of merging them into one impact score. AlphaFold 3 and the Boltz models now predict complex structures accurately enough to make this comparison practical at scale [4–6].
Several tools place variants on experimental or predicted structures [7–13], and two of them consider both monomeric and assembled contexts. ELASPIC assigns each substitution to the core or to an interface and predicts the corresponding quantity [14]. MAVISp computes folding and local-interaction free energies in separate modules for more than a thousand proteins [15]. Neither scores the same substitution in the isolated subunit and in the assembled complex and compares the two, and that comparison carries mechanistic information. A substitution that destabilizes the isolated subunit predicts a protein that folds poorly and is depleted; one that leaves the subunit intact but weakens an interaction predicts a protein that is present but fails to engage its partner. The first calls for a stability or turnover assay, the second for an affinity measurement against the named partner. A single energy cannot separate the two, because a residue buried in an interface can destabilize the fold without weakening binding. Stability and interface predictors have been benchmarked against measured energies and deep mutational scans [16–23], but such benchmarks score energies, not the identification of the disrupted process.
Here we describe COMAVI (COMplex-Aware Variant Impact scoring), a pipeline that scores each substitution in both contexts and keeps its fold and binding predictions separate (Fig 1). COMAVI returns two linked outputs: a priority score, which ranks variants for structural follow-up, and a mechanism profile, which names the process predicted to be disrupted. To test both, we curated a benchmark of 61 variants in 14 protein systems in which every expected effect is tied to a written evidence basis and a citation. We asked whether the mechanism profile recovers the literature-defined mechanism, whether the priority score concentrates structural mechanisms at the top of a ranking, and how both answers depend on the energy threshold that turns a continuous prediction into a call. We then compared the predicted energies with direct measurements and the priority score with a pathogenicity predictor.
Fig 1. COMAVI workflow. A missense variant and isolated and assembled structural models yield four lines of structural evidence: predicted stability changes in the monomer (isolated subunit) and in the multimer (assembled complex), the binding-energy change for each partner, and the structural-context tier. These combine into two outputs. The priority score ranks the strength of modeled structural disruption and is used to choose variants for further study; the mechanism profile assigns each variant its predicted mechanisms and context and indicates whether to test stability, assembly or interaction. Pathogenicity evidence is not an input to either output.
Results
Every expectation in the benchmark carries its evidence
The benchmark contains 61 missense variants in 14 protein systems (Table 1): 49 variants in 13 multi-chain complexes and 12 in the tandem BRCA1 C-terminal (BRCT) domain, a single-chain system with measured unfolding free energies [24]. We chose systems in which published experiments establish how each variant acts. Together they span the mechanisms COMAVI is meant to separate: loss and gain of function, fold-driven and binding-driven disruption, and variants with ambiguous biology. For each variant, the primary literature defines the expected direction on each applicable axis [25–58]. Each of the 98 committed expectations is recorded with its evidence class, a one-line basis and a citation (S3 Table).
Table 1. Benchmark composition.
Graded variants carry a committed expectation on at least one modeled axis or, for five no-lesion variants, at the whole-variant level. Pathogenic counts clinical annotations, which are independent of the mechanism ground truth and are not used in any mechanism metric; the BRCT variants carry no clinical label (–). Experimental structures are identified by their Protein Data Bank (PDB) entries [59–64]; the other systems used AlphaFold 3 models.
Three nested populations serve different claims. All 61 variants receive COMAVI outputs. Of these, 57 are graded against an expected mechanism: 25 carry a committed structural mechanism and 32 are curated to carry no lesion on the modeled axes, which we call no-lesion variants. Four variants are not graded. The mechanism of BRCA1 R1699L and R1699Q, loss of phosphopeptide recognition, cannot be represented by the supplied structure, and TNNI3 R162W and VHL W117R have no committed expectation on any modeled axis (S1 Table, model_scope sheet). Of the 57 graded variants, the 47 in complexes also carry a structural-context tier and form the prioritization population of 17 structural-mechanism and 30 no-lesion variants. The 17 are a subset of the 25; the other eight are BRCT fold mechanisms, which have no partner chain and therefore no tier.
Five graded no-lesion variants carry no committed expectation on any individual axis: CALM1 D96V, N98S and F142L, which act through altered calcium binding [29,30], and SMAD4 I500T and I500V, which act through impaired monoubiquitination [44]. Their mechanisms lie outside the modeled axes, so they are graded at the whole-variant level as no-lesion variants. S3 Table lists their basis and sources, and the analyses below report the effect of removing them.
Evidence quality differs by direction, because a disruptive effect attracts a dedicated biophysical measurement far more often than a normal one does. Of the 38 expectations supported by quantitative evidence (classes E1 and E2; Methods), 25 are destabilizing; of the 60 supported by qualitative, population or inferred evidence (E3 to E5), 8 are. All 9 population-supported and all 18 inferred expectations are neutral. Restricting the benchmark to the best-evidenced expectations would therefore remove most of the no-lesion class instead of raising the standard evenly. We retained every evidence class and report evidence-restricted analyses as sensitivity analyses (S1 Text).
COMAVI recovers literature-defined mechanisms but localizes them less reliably than it detects them
Each graded variant received a three-level grade: 1 when COMAVI recovered the expected combination of effects, 0.5 when it partly recovered a multi-axis pattern, and 0 otherwise. Table 2 defines this mechanism-pattern score and every other metric reported below. At the 2.5 kcal/mol (10.5 kJ/mol) reference threshold, the mechanism-pattern score was 41.0 of 57 (0.719). To judge this value, we graded every trivial fixed strategy on the same variants. The strongest, calling every variant silent, scores 0.561 by accepting all no-lesion variants and missing every mechanism. Calling every variant destabilized on every axis scores 0.386, and permuting the ground truth gives a mean of 0.484 (standard deviation 0.052; p = 0.0005; S2 Text). COMAVI exceeds the best trivial strategy by 0.158.
Table 2. Metrics, populations and values at each energy threshold.
Values at the four uniform thresholds (kcal/mol) and at the per-axis prediction-interval bound (2.9, 2.9 and 3.5 kcal/mol for isolated-subunit stability, assembled-complex stability and binding). Detection, attribution and correct rejection use every computed axis before confidence gating; the rubric grades use confidence-passing axes only. ROC AUC (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve) uses no threshold and takes one value.
The pooled score combines two capabilities that behave differently. The grade averaged 0.600 over the 25 structural-mechanism variants and 0.812 over the 32 no-lesion variants. Asked as binary questions, the same calls give three rates. At least one axis reached the threshold for 20 of the 25 mechanisms (detection, 0.800), and the axis the literature implicates reached it for 14 (attribution, 0.560). No axis reached it for 26 of the 32 no-lesion variants (correct rejection, 0.812). Binary attribution agrees with the graded structural arm to within 0.040 at every uniform threshold, so partial credit does not inflate the structural arm. COMAVI therefore detects structural disruption more reliably than it localizes it, and this gap is its principal limitation.
The localization errors concentrate in one class. Among the nine variants curated as pure interface destabilizers, the binding axis fired for 4 and the assembled-complex stability axis for 7. The lesion was found but placed in the fold of the complex instead of in the interaction (S2 Text). Two variants show both what the second context adds and this error. Complement factor H R78G barely changes isolated-subunit stability (0.93 kcal/mol) but is predicted to weaken binding to C3b by 6.12 kcal/mol, consistent with a measured rise in dissociation constant from 10–14 μM to more than 35 μM [51]. Von Willebrand factor R1334Q leaves the isolated A1 domain unchanged (0.01 kcal/mol) but weakens binding to GPIbα by 4.11 kcal/mol; the purified A1 domain is natively folded, binds GPIbα weakly and supports little platelet adhesion [52]. A calculation on the isolated subunit alone would call both variants silent. In both, however, the assembled-complex stability axis also fires (4.88 and 4.73 kcal/mol) where the literature implies an intact fold, so both are graded partial.
Direction agreement was similar on the three axes: 19 of 25 expectations for isolated-subunit stability, 17 of 22 for assembled-complex stability and 22 of 30 for binding (58 of 77 overall), rates within 0.040 of one another. The 47 complex variants and the 10 BRCT variants scored similarly (0.723 and 0.700), so neither subset determines the pooled result.
No single protein system carries the result. Resampling whole systems gave a 95% interval of 0.625–0.821 for the mechanism-pattern score, and leaving out one system at a time gave values from 0.704 to 0.750 (S1 Fig). Without the five whole-variant no-lesion variants, the score is 36.0 of 52 (0.692). Because the 57 variants come from 14 clustered systems, 0.719 measures agreement on this benchmark, not general accuracy.
A silent result has two meanings that the benchmark keeps apart. For a variant whose mechanism the supplied model can represent, silence on every applicable axis is an informative negative. When the mechanism acts through a state absent from the model, silence cannot test it. HBB E6V is the clearest case: it acts through polymerization of deoxygenated hemoglobin [53], so COMAVI silence points to the missing assembly state, not to a benign variant. A model-scope screen separates the two situations (S1 Table, model_scope sheet).
The priority score concentrates structural mechanisms at the top of the ranking
The priority score combines a context component and an energy component (Methods), and we first tested each alone on the 47-variant prioritization population. The structural-context tier uses no energy. It placed all 17 structural-mechanism variants in Tier 1 or 2 (sensitivity 1.000, 95% confidence interval 0.805–1.000) together with 13 of the 30 no-lesion variants (specificity 0.567, 0.374–0.745; within-system permutation p = 0.0095). The tier is therefore permissive, and because it cannot identify which process is affected, it never overrides an axis energy. Interface status alone was more specific (0.767) and recovered 15 of the 17 mechanisms; the full tier also recovered two structural-mechanism variants outside any detected interface (S1 Table, context_comparators sheet; S3 Fig). On the 26 variants whose every committed axis has E1 to E3 support, sensitivity was unchanged and specificity was 0.615 (S1 Text).
We measured ranking quality by the ROC AUC, the probability that a randomly chosen structural-mechanism variant outranks a randomly chosen no-lesion variant. The energy component alone reached an AUC of 0.857 and placed 15 of the 17 mechanisms in the top 20, but no more by rank 25. It ranks HBB N102T 37th and MLH1 Q542L 33rd of 47, although the tier places both in Tier 1. The context component alone reached 0.890 and recovered 14 mechanisms in the top 20 and 16 by rank 25. The two components fail on different variants. Crossing the energy calls with the tier shows the same complementarity: at 1.0 and at 2.5 kcal/mol, no structural-mechanism variant falls in the energy-only or the neither state. At 2.5 kcal/mol, 15 mechanisms are convergent and 2 context-only, whereas the energy-only state holds 3 no-lesion variants (8 at 1.0 kcal/mol) and no mechanism (S2 Table, evidence_states sheet). In this cohort, an energy call without supporting structural context marks a no-lesion variant.
Combining the two components recovered what neither reaches alone (Fig 2). The priority score placed 9 of the 17 structural mechanisms in the top 10, 16 in the top 20 and all 17 in the top 25. Its ROC AUC was 0.943 and its average precision 0.818, against a positive rate of 17 in 47; without the five whole-variant no-lesion variants, the AUC was 0.939. The ranking applies no energy threshold, and its positive class comes from the curated mechanism class, so none of these values depends on the threshold.
Fig 2. The priority score concentrates structural mechanisms at the top of the ranking. (A) Priority scores of the 17 structural-mechanism and 30 no-lesion variants in the 47-variant prioritization population. (B) Structural mechanisms recovered as a function of rank depth for the priority score and for its context and energy components ranked alone.
A high rank does not guarantee a correct mechanism. Of the 16 structural mechanisms in the top 20, 5 are graded consistent, 9 partial and 2 inconsistent; the only mechanism outside the top 20, HBB W37Y at rank 21, is graded partial. The two inconsistent cases, MLH1 R755W at rank 11 and PIK3CA E545K at rank 15, fail the same way. FoldX returns a large favorable interaction energy for each (−9.7 and −4.6 kcal/mol), which COMAVI reads as strengthened binding. For E545K the axis is right but the direction is wrong, because releasing the inhibitory contact with p85 is energetically favorable [40]. For R755W the literature reports reduced abundance with binding preserved [32,36], and neither stability prediction reaches the threshold (0.97 kcal/mol for the isolated subunit and −0.94 in the complex). These two variants would send a user to the right protein with the wrong experiment (S3 Text).
Stringency costs attribution, not detection
Every graded result depends on the energy threshold that turns a continuous prediction into a call (Fig 3). Over the prespecified uniform thresholds of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5 kcal/mol and the per-axis prediction-interval bound (Methods), the mechanism-pattern score was 0.579, 0.649, 0.684, 0.719 and 0.711. The 2.5 kcal/mol reference therefore maximizes the score on this cohort, as it does the unweighted mean of the two arms, but the system-resampling intervals overlap at every threshold (Fig 3C).

(A) Detection, attribution and correct rejection for the 25 structural-mechanism and 32 no-lesion variants. (B) Direction agreement on each axis (25 isolated-subunit, 22 assembled-complex and 30 binding expectations); open circles mark the best value for each axis. (C) Mechanism-pattern score, pooled (gray band, 95% interval from resampling the 14 systems) and for each arm. (D) Measured destabilizations of at least 1.0 kcal/mol (n = 42) predicted at or above the threshold with the same sign.
Raising the threshold changed the three binary rates in different ways. Detection was nearly flat: at least one axis fired for 23 of the 25 mechanisms at 1.0 kcal/mol, for 20 at 1.5 to 2.5 kcal/mol and for 18 at the bound. Attribution fell at every step, from 19 to 17, 16, 14 and 12. Correct rejection rose at every step, from 5 of 32 to 19, 23, 26 and 27. Stringency therefore buys rejection by losing the correct axis, not by missing the lesion. The structural arm fell from 0.760 to 0.540 and the no-lesion arm rose from 0.438 to 0.844, and the two cross between 1.5 and 2.0 kcal/mol (Fig 3A and 3C).
The three axes prefer different thresholds (Fig 3B). Binding agreement was highest at 1.0 and 1.5 kcal/mol (24 of 30) and fell to 22 at the reference. Assembled-complex agreement rose from 12 of 22 at 1.0 kcal/mol to 17 at the reference, and isolated-subunit agreement peaked at 2.0 and 2.5 kcal/mol (19 of 25). The denominators do not change across thresholds. A single global threshold is therefore set where the two stability axes perform best, and binding gives up 0.067 relative to its own optimum. Per-axis thresholds are the most promising single change to the calling layer, because they would recover part of the interface shortfall.
Measured effects show the same cost of stringency. We counted the 42 comparisons with a measured destabilization of at least 1.0 kcal/mol, 13 from benchmark variants and 29 from external measurements, and asked whether FoldX predicted a destabilization of at least the threshold with the same sign. It did for 27 at 1.0 kcal/mol, 12 at 2.5 kcal/mol and 11 at the bound (Fig 3D). A stringent threshold can therefore miss a real effect even when the measured destabilization is large.
Predicted energies rank measured effects but compress their size
The thresholds act on predicted free-energy changes (ΔΔG), so we compared predictions with direct measurements wherever they exist (Fig 4). The pooled set contains 63 comparisons from four systems: 48 binding energies for barnase–barstar and TEM1–BLIP from SKEMPI 2.0 [65], 10 unfolding free energies for the BRCA1 BRCT domain [24] and 5 hemoglobin assembly energies [54–56]. Predicted and measured values were rank-correlated (Spearman ρ = 0.48, p = 5.6 × 10−5), but the fitted slope was 0.42, and the prediction error became more negative as the measured effect grew (ρ = −0.66). A measured destabilization of 2.5 kcal/mol corresponds to a predicted 1.43 kcal/mol. FoldX therefore orders effects but compresses large ones, which accounts for part of the loss of measured effects at stringent thresholds.
Fig 4. Predicted energies rank measured effects but compress their magnitude. (A) Predicted against measured ΔΔG for 63 comparisons from four systems, with the least-squares fit (slope 0.42) and the identity line; open symbols mark the seven barnase Glu73 substitutions. (B) Prediction error (predicted minus measured) against measured ΔΔG. (C) Isolated-subunit stability for the 10 graded BRCA1 BRCT variants against measured unfolding free energy. (D) Binding energy against measured assembly free energy for the five hemoglobin β-chain benchmark variants at the α1β2 interface. Dotted lines mark 2.5 kcal/mol; positive values are destabilizing.
Seven barnase substitutions at Glu73 behave differently from the rest. All are measured as destabilizing (1.45 to 3.01 kcal/mol) and all are predicted as stabilizing (−1.66 to −1.01 kcal/mol), a systematic sign inversion at a single site. They are included in every primary statistic. Without them the rank correlation rises to 0.63 (n = 56) while the slope barely changes (0.43), so the compression does not depend on this cluster. For the SKEMPI binding comparisons alone, ρ = 0.34 (p = 0.019, n = 48), and the predicted sign was correct for 30 of 40 measured destabilizations. Without Glu73, ρ = 0.55 (p = 0.0002, n = 41), and the sign was correct for 30 of 33 (S2 Fig). At the 2.5 kcal/mol reference, measured and predicted calls agreed for 43 of the 63 comparisons (S5 Fig).
Two axes also have direct anchors within the benchmark's own systems. Predicted isolated-subunit stability tracked measured unfolding free energy across the 10 graded BRCT variants (ρ = 0.72; Fig 4C). The only one measured above 2.5 kcal/mol but predicted below it is V1736A (measured 4.20, predicted 1.24 kcal/mol). Predicted binding tracked measured hemoglobin assembly energies (ρ = 0.90, n = 5; Fig 4D). The largest measured penalty, βW37E at 9.00 kcal/mol, was predicted at 5.14 kcal/mol, so magnitude is underestimated without changing any call at 2.5 kcal/mol. The benchmark contains no direct measurement of assembled-complex stability, so that axis is tested only against literature-defined directions.
These comparisons support reporting the sign, rank and continuous value of each predicted energy with its replicate spread and threshold. They do not support treating a FoldX ΔΔG as a measured energy.
Structural prioritization and pathogenicity prediction answer different questions
Because the priority score ranks structural disruption, it invites comparison with pathogenicity predictors. We made that comparison on the 47 complex variants that have both a clinical label and an AlphaMissense score (36 pathogenic, 11 benign). This set differs from the prioritization population. It includes TNNI3 R162W and VHL W117R, which are not graded, and lacks VWF R1334Q and A1381T, for which no AlphaMissense score was retrieved. AlphaMissense ranked pathogenicity better (ROC AUC 0.903) than the priority score (0.851) or its energy (0.836) and context (0.769) components (Fig 5). This is the expected result. AlphaMissense asks whether a variant is likely to be pathogenic; the priority score asks how strongly the structure supports a modeled disruption, and these pathogenicity AUCs are not comparable with the structural AUC of 0.943.

AlphaMissense pathogenicity against (A) the priority score and (B) its energy component for the 47 complex variants with a clinical label and an AlphaMissense score. Filled circles, pathogenic (including gain of function); open circles, benign; gray band, the AlphaMissense ambiguous range (0.34–0.564). Labelled variants are discussed in the text.
Discordant variants show what each view adds. HBB E6V is pathogenic but scores low on both AlphaMissense (0.223) and the priority score (0.275), because its mechanism is absent from the supplied model. CFH R78G has a high priority score (0.818) and a named binding lesion. PIK3CA H1047R and TNNI3 R162W sit in Tier 2 context, but neither has an energy call on any axis at the reference threshold, so COMAVI ranks them on context alone and names no mechanism. All three of these clinically pathogenic variants, R78G, H1047R and R162W, fall in the AlphaMissense ambiguous range (0.398, 0.538 and 0.411), as does one benign variant, MSH2 N127S. Where the pathogenicity score is least decided, the structural profile supplies a named mechanism for one variant and a place to look for two.
Silent results occurred in both pathogenic and benign variants (S3 Text), so silence does not separate them. The asymmetry matters for use: a structural call supplies a testable hypothesis, whereas a silent result is weak evidence against pathogenicity, because the mechanism may lie outside what the model represents.
Discussion
Using the two outputs
COMAVI answers two practical questions: which variants deserve structural follow-up, and which experiment should test the predicted effect. Its two outputs fail differently. The priority score uses no threshold, so the prioritization results hold at any threshold, whereas the mechanism profile depends on the threshold at which a continuous energy becomes a call. A practical workflow therefore has two steps. The priority score allocates experimental effort. The signed energies for isolated-subunit stability, assembled-complex stability and binding to each partner, read with their uncertainty, threshold, tier and model limitations, then select a stability, assembly or binding experiment. A high score without an energy call is a reason to examine a site, not a named lesion.
The threshold should follow the cost of error. The 1.0 kcal/mol default suits screening, where a missed mechanism costs more than extra follow-up; 2.5 kcal/mol is the benchmark reference; and the prediction-interval bound suits settings where a false mechanistic claim is costly. These thresholds separate curated mechanisms from no-lesion variants, a different question from the 3 kcal/mol cut-off that MAVISp and related MLH1 studies use to predict cellular degradation [15,66,67]. Because each call is reported with its continuous energy, a user can re-read it at another threshold; 21 of the 57 graded variants change grade within the prespecified range (S1 Table, benchmark_variants sheet). A silent result means that the supplied static model shows no lesion at the stated threshold. It does not mean that a variant is benign, nonfunctional or unworthy of study.
COMAVI complements pathogenicity predictors, population data, functional assays and clinical interpretation tools such as Franklin [68]. Shown side by side, a pathogenicity score and the COMAVI outputs disagree in interpretable ways (Fig 5). Combining them into one score would reintroduce the conflation that separating structure from pathogenicity is meant to remove.
Relation to existing frameworks
MAVISp is the closest existing framework [15]. Both use FoldX BuildModel for stability and AnalyseComplex for interaction energy, averaged over replicates, and both report fold and binding effects separately; MAVISp also runs a consensus of predictors and covers more than a thousand proteins. COMAVI differs in three ways. First, it scores each substitution in both the isolated subunit and the assembled complex and contrasts the two, whereas the MAVISp stability module uses one structure per target and its interaction module treats interfaces separately. Second, it evaluates mechanism attribution against literature-defined expectations for each axis and against measured energies. MAVISp is validated against functional assays and clinical labels, and its authors note that interaction data are too scarce and heterogeneous to benchmark its binding module. The two designs trade scale against the availability of a measured answer. Third, COMAVI is a pipeline that runs on a user's own structures and variants, whereas MAVISp is a curated database to which new proteins are added. ELASPIC likewise assigns each substitution to a core or an interface model [14].
The work makes three separable contributions. The first is the isolated-versus-assembled contrast, which can separate a predicted folding problem from a predicted binding problem. The second is a benchmark, usable independently of COMAVI, in which every expectation carries a written basis and a citation and an automated check enforces their agreement. The third is the comparison of predictions with measured energies instead of with other predictors.
Limitations and next tests
FoldX preserves the direction and rank of an effect better than its size. Published correlations between FoldX and measured stability changes range from 0.19 to 0.81, and the uncertainty of a single prediction is large [69]; evaluations report qualitative agreement more often than quantitative agreement [16]. Here the slope against measured energies was 0.42. Large destabilizations are therefore understated, stringent thresholds miss real effects, and the priority score cannot be read in experimental energy units.
A related caution applies to small effects. A variant below the threshold is reported as carrying no modeled lesion on that axis, not as having no effect. Modest destabilizations can matter, especially where a small change in stability or affinity alters assembly or turnover. At 2.5 kcal/mol COMAVI reproduced 12 of 42 measured destabilizations, against 27 at 1.0 kcal/mol, so a study concerned with mild perturbations should use the lower threshold and accept more false calls.
The benchmark is small and clustered: 57 graded variants in 14 systems, with the variants of a system sharing structures and curation. Resampling whole systems addresses this dependence but cannot replace prospective validation. Evidence is also uneven: isolated-subunit stability and binding have direct energy anchors, assembled-complex stability has none, and five no-lesion variants are graded only at the whole-variant level.
COMAVI evaluates static coordinates. It cannot capture catalytic, dynamic, aggregative, regulatory, post-translational, ligand-dependent or other state-dependent mechanisms, so model limitations must be assessed before a silent result is interpreted.
The most informative next tests are external panels that separate folding from binding and include both disruptive and function-retaining variants. More variants in the VWF A1–GPIbα and complement factor H–C3b systems would strengthen the evidence on fold-neutral binding lesions, and SOD1, transthyretin and the dense PDZ and SH3 landscapes can test coupled tertiary, quaternary and allosteric effects [3,70,71]. The priority-score formula is frozen and should be evaluated on such panels without retuning. Extending the tier and the priority score to monomer-only systems would require a prespecified formula and prospective validation.
COMAVI turns a list of missense variants into a fixed priority for structural follow-up and a mechanism profile that proposes the disrupted process and the experiment to test it. On this benchmark it recovered most literature-defined mechanisms, ranked structural mechanisms near the top, and failed most often by placing a detected lesion on the wrong axis. Its value lies in directing experiments, not in predicting pathogenicity.
Materials and methods
COMAVI outputs
COMAVI takes a missense variant and a structural model of its complex and calculates three FoldX energy changes: the stability of the isolated subunit, the stability calculated on the assembled coordinates and the interaction energy with each partner. A structural-context tier summarizes the local environment of the site without using energy. From these, COMAVI returns the priority score, which ranks variants by the strength of modeled structural disruption, and the mechanism profile, which keeps the signed energies, the tier, their uncertainty, the threshold and the model limitations. A profile may support an isolated-subunit effect, an assembled-complex effect, a binding effect, more than one of these, or none. The energies, the tier and the priority score are continuous. A threshold is needed only to turn the energies into the discrete calls that are graded against categorical literature expectations, so the threshold belongs to this evaluation, not to the method.
Structures and energy calculations
COMAVI scores a variant on a supplied model of a complex whose composition is known; it does not discover partners. Complex structures were predicted with the AlphaFold 3 server [4], and seven systems used experimental structures (Table 1). Energies were calculated with FoldX 5.1 [72,73] using default energy-function parameters. Energies are reported in kcal/mol, the convention of the stability literature; 1 kcal/mol is 4.184 kJ/mol. Each structure was repaired once with RepairPDB. BuildModel was run five times per variant on the isolated subunit and on the assembled complex, and AnalyseComplex was applied to each mutant complex to obtain the change in interaction energy with each partner. ΔΔG is mutant minus wild type in kcal/mol, so positive values are destabilizing; the axis value is the mean over replicates, and the replicate standard deviation is kept. Summary outputs use the most disruptive partner, and partner-level values remain available. Running BuildModel and AnalyseComplex separately keeps an interface-localized fold signal from being read as a binding change. S1 Text gives the commands.
Structural confidence
FoldX calculates ΔΔG from the supplied coordinates, and the predicted local distance difference test (pLDDT) of an AlphaFold model does not rescale these energies. Instead, pLDDT decides whether an axis from a predicted structure can support a mechanism call or enter the priority score, using the confidence bands of the AlphaFold database [74]. Sites with pLDDT ≥ 70 count as confident. Sites from 50 to below 70 are retained as lower-confidence evidence when axis-specific uncertainty checks also pass. Below 50, the predicted axis is not used, and the partner contributes no contacts, interface occupancy or burial to the tier. The tier's context score is multiplied by 0.7 for a best-site pLDDT from 50 to below 70 and by 0.4 below 50; experimental structures are not attenuated. Isolated-subunit stability is not graded in regions without an independently stable fold.
Structural-context tier and priority score
The structural-context tier describes how strongly a site is embedded in features that make it sensitive to substitution. It contains no energy and is not a mechanism call. It combines substitution severity, scored by the Grantham distance [75], with local contacts, interface occupancy, burial and model confidence. The fixed total maps to Tier 1 (strongest context) through Tier 4 (weakest), and S1 Text gives the weights and cut-offs. Because interface occupancy contributes directly to the tier, we compared the full tier with interface status alone and with the tier recalculated without its interface bonus (S2 Text).
The integrated structural-disruption score (ISDS-v1), called the priority score here, combines energy and context with a fixed formula: the same reference values and tier mapping apply to every variant set, and no coefficient is fitted to the cohort. For each confidence-passing energy j, |ΔΔGj| is divided by a reference value τj of 2.9 kcal/mol for the two stability axes and 3.5 kcal/mol for binding. These are the upper bounds of the published FoldX 95% prediction intervals [69]. Here they serve as divisors that put the three axes on one scale, not as cut-offs, so an energy below τj still contributes. Energy thresholds and evidence states
A predicted energy becomes a call on an axis when its absolute value reaches the threshold. Five operating points were fixed before scoring: uniform thresholds of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5 kcal/mol, and the prediction-interval bound. The bound takes, separately for each axis, the upper limit of the 95% prediction interval fitted to FoldX folding and binding predictions [69]: 2.9 kcal/mol for isolated-subunit stability, 2.9 for assembled-complex stability and 3.5 for binding. A call that survives the bound is unlikely to arise from prediction error alone. The released software uses 1.0 kcal/mol as a screening default, and 2.5 kcal/mol is the benchmark reference. S2 Table (threshold_sweep sheet) reports post hoc points above the bound. Crossing the energy calls with the tier (Tier 1–2 against Tier 3–4) defines four descriptive evidence states: convergent, energy only, context only and neither (S2 Text).
Benchmark curation
The primary literature defined the expected direction on each applicable axis, and each expectation was classified by what its source measured. Class E1, quantitative energetic, is a reported thermodynamic or kinetic quantity for the substitution, such as a melting-temperature shift, an unfolding free energy or a dissociation constant. E2, quantitative functional, is a numerical assay readout that stands in for one of these, such as a deep-mutational-scanning abundance or interaction score. E3, qualitative experimental, is a direct observation reported without a number, such as loss of co-immunoprecipitation. E4, population frequency, is inference from allele frequency or constraint without an assay of the variant. E5, inferred, is an expectation implied by an established mechanism without a measurement of that axis; a variant shown to bind normally while losing catalytic activity, for example, implies a neutral binding axis. The absence of an assay was never treated as evidence: without a published result implying the axis state, the axis was left uncommitted. S3 Table gives the class, basis and citation of all 98 committed expectations, and an automated check enforces agreement between each committed direction and its written basis (S3 Text).
For each variant, we asked whether the supplied structure contains the context its known mechanism needs: the relevant partner, ligand, modification, oligomeric state and conformation. When the context was absent, a silent result could not test the mechanism and was treated as a limitation of the model, not as negative evidence. The isolated BRCT structure, for example, represents fold energetics but not phosphopeptide recognition by R1699L and R1699Q; HBB E6V requires the deoxygenated polymerization state; VHL Y98H lacks the bound hypoxia-inducible factor peptide; and CALM1 N98S depends on calcium-linked dynamics. S1 Table (model_scope sheet) gives the rules.
Outcomes and statistics
The mechanism-pattern score is the mean of a three-level grade (Table 3): 1 for a fully consistent whole-variant pattern, 0.5 for a partly correct multi-axis pattern and 0 for an inconsistent pattern. It is a weighted mechanism consistency, not exact-match accuracy, and unknown or inapplicable axes are omitted. Direction agreement counts, for each axis, the expectations whose call matches the committed direction, irrespective of the size of the predicted energy. Detection, attribution and correct rejection ask the same question as binary rates (Table 2).
Table 3. Whole-variant grading rubric.
Each graded variant receives one grade at each threshold; the mechanism-pattern score is the mean grade.
A measured destabilization counts as recovered at a threshold when the predicted energy reaches that threshold with the same sign; the population is every comparison with a measured destabilization of at least 1.0 kcal/mol. Calibration used Spearman correlation and least-squares regression of predicted on measured ΔΔG.
For prioritization, the positive class is the 17 structural-mechanism variants and the negative class the 30 no-lesion variants in the 47-variant population. Average precision summarizes precision over the recall curve, and top-k recovery counts the structural mechanisms among the k highest-ranked variants. Alternative formulas, score bands and component analyses are internal sensitivity analyses, because the same benchmark informed the development of the score (S2 Text; S4 Fig). Within-system permutation tests for the tier screens used exact convolved hypergeometric distributions.
Variants in a system share structures and curation, so the main uncertainty analysis resampled whole systems (2,000 draws). We also report leave-one-system-out values, variant-level bootstrap intervals, label permutations and replicate noise; S1 Text lists the seeds and draw counts. Confidence intervals for proportions are exact binomial intervals.

S1 Text. Implementation details, statistical conventions and evidence provenance.
Section 1. Implementation details and statistical conventions
Each structural context was repaired once and cached. Variants were specified in FoldX notation. BuildModel was run five times on both isolated and assembled coordinates, and AnalyzeComplex was applied pairwise to each resulting mutant complex. Beyond the command, input, replicate count, partner chains, and output directory, no FoldX energy-function parameter was overridden.
foldx --command=RepairPDB --pdb=<structure> --output-dir=<workdir>
foldx --command=BuildModel --pdb=<repaired> --mutant-file=individual_list.txt --numberOfRuns=5 --output-dir=<workdir>
foldx --command=AnalyzeComplex --pdb=<mutant_complex> --analyzeComplexChains=<chainA>,<chainB> --output-dir=<workdir>
The mutant-minus-wild-type ΔΔG was read from FoldX outputs. Wild-type interaction energy was computed once per partner pair, and mutant interaction energy was computed on each of the five BuildModel replicates. Partner-level binding ΔΔG is the mean of IE(mutant) - IE(wild type); replicate standard deviation is retained.
Structural-context tier implementation. The raw score is Grantham substitution severity [53] multiplied by the sum of monomer contacts and the maximum confidence-passing inter-chain contact count, plus an interface bonus (1.5 for one partner; 2.0 for at least two) and a burial bonus (1.0 for partial burial; 2.0 for a buried core position). The result is multiplied by 1.0 for pLDDT at least 70, 0.7 for pLDDT 50-70, and 0.4 below 50; experimental structures bypass this attenuation. Fixed cutoffs assign Tier 1 at s at least 5.0, Tier 2 at 3.0 to less than 5.0, Tier 3 at 1.5 to less than 3.0, and Tier 4 below 1.5.
Section 2. Evidence provenance and denominator sensitivity
The evidence ledger contains 98 committed energetic-axis expectations. E1-E3 expectations are supported by energetic, functional, or qualitative experiments. E4 denotes population-supported neutral expectations. E5 denotes an inferred axis state used when the literature supported a mechanistic or retained-function interpretation but no axis-specific assay was available; inference was not assigned from missing data alone. E4 and E5 commitments are heavily enriched for neutral expectations: all 9 E4 axes and all 18 E5 axes are neutral. Complex-context stability is the least independently grounded axis: 14 of its 25 committed axes rest on inferred rather than direct or coupled evidence, and 11 are E5 inferred axis states.
Using the all-row 78-axis convention, energetic-output agreement was 20/32 = 0.625 for E1-E2 evidence and 38/46 = 0.826 for E3-E5 evidence (Fisher p = 0.065). Evidence class was strongly associated with expected axis state: direct measurements were preferentially available for destabilizers, whereas frequency-supported and inferred commitments were predominantly neutral. Within expected-destabilizing axes, agreement was 14/24 for E1-E2 and 4/6 for E3-E5; within expected-neutral axes, the corresponding rates were 6/7 and 34/40. The continuity-corrected Mantel-Haenszel estimate across expected-state strata was odds ratio 0.83 (p = 0.92). Thus the current benchmark does not show that evidence category independently explains energetic-output agreement after expected axis state is considered. Among 25 gradeable E1 energetic axes, 18 agreed (0.720).
Strict variant-level sensitivity analyses used all-axis definitions. Requiring every committed axis to have E1-E3 support left n = 26, 13 structural and 13 no-lesion (tier sensitivity 1.000, 95% CI 0.753-1.000; specificity 0.615, 0.316-0.861). Requiring every committed axis to be direct or coupled left the same 26 variants and returned the same values. Complete recovery here rests on 13 positives, so the interval is wide; it is reported because the restriction is a sensitivity view of the full-tier screen, which recovers all 17 positives on the whole subset.

S2 Text. Sensitivity analyses, mechanism-class decomposition and operating-point diagnostics.
Section 1. Mechanism-class decomposition, fold-arm errors, and ground-truth conventions
Weighted mechanism consistency differed by expected mechanism class: 0.833 for mixed structural expectations (n = 6), 0.812 for curated structurally silent variants (n = 32), 0.550 for the fold-mechanism class (n = 10), and 0.500 for pure interface destabilization (n = 9). The interface class is therefore the weakest arm. Fold-arm errors separate into prediction misses and disagreements created by the sign-based ground-truth convention.
Section 2. Operating-point and calibration diagnostics
The apparent pooled optimum depends on class mixture. The benchmark contains 25 variants with committed structural mechanisms and 32 benchmark negatives, a structural fraction of 0.439. Reweighting the classes moved the pooled optimum: 2.5 kcal/mol remained the pooled argmax only while the weight on the structural arm stayed at or below 0.645, well above the benchmark's own structural fraction, so the reference point is not an artifact of this cohort's class mixture, though it would move under a substantially more structural-heavy one. This analysis supports reporting a threshold menu rather than a universal optimum.
In 10,000 paired whole-system resamples, in which the same resample of the 14 systems is scored at every threshold, graded recovery decreased from 0.757 (95% CI 0.639-0.861) at 1.0 kcal/mol to 0.536 (0.405-0.679) at the prediction-interval bound, whereas correct rejection increased from 0.445 (0.270-0.625) to 0.849 (0.688-1.000). The probability that recovery fell was 0.999, and the probability that rejection rose was 1.000. The direction of the curves is forced by thresholding, however; resampling quantifies the size of the exchange, not its existence.
The mechanism-attribution menu also answers a different question from the 3 kcal/mol fold cutoff used for cellular-stability or degradation interpretation in MAVISp [15] and related MLH1 studies [54,55]. A predicted fold change below that cellular-abundance cutoff can still identify the correct structural axis for follow-up. We therefore retain the 3 kcal/mol comparison as endpoint context, not as a basis for selecting a COMAVI operating point.
Section 3. Structural-context comparators, evidence states, and priority-score sensitivity
The full tier, an interface-only screen, and the tier without its interface bonus were compared on the same 47-variant prioritization subset (S1 Table, context_comparators sheet; S3 Fig). The full tier recovered all 17 structural mechanisms (exact 95% CI 0.805-1.000) but also flagged 13 of 30 no-lesion variants, giving specificity 0.567 (95% CI 0.374-0.745). Complete recovery on seventeen variants is compatible with a true sensitivity as low as 0.805, so the interval rather than the point estimate is what the screen supports. Interface status alone recovered 15/17 and rejected 23/30; the no-interface tier recovered 14/17 and rejected 18/30. Exact within-system permutation p values were 0.0095, 0.0018, and 0.129, respectively. Per-tier structural fractions were 11/13 in Tier 1, 6/17 in Tier 2, and 0/17 across Tiers 3-4, so the gradient is now strictly decreasing and no variant with a modeled structural mechanism falls in the two weakest tiers. Removing the interface bonus demoted HBB W37Y, PIK3CA E542K, PIK3CA E545K, and CALM1 D96V from Tier 2 to Tier 3.
Priority-score bands. Ranking performance is summarised above as a single area under the curve, which says nothing about where on the scale the mechanisms sit. Partitioning the 47-variant subset into five equal score bands shows the concentration directly: 8 of the 9 variants in the highest band (0.80-1.00) carry a modeled structural mechanism, 6 of 8 in the next band (0.60-<0.80), and 0 of 16 across the two lowest bands combined. The scale is therefore usable as a magnitude and not only as an ordering.
Leave-one-system-out influence. Because variants cluster within protein systems, a single system could carry the ranking result. Removing each of the 13 systems represented in this subset in turn and recomputing gives priority-score ROC AUC between 0.929 and 0.984, against 0.943 on the full subset. In all 13 folds the combined score ranked above both of its own components, so the advantage of combining energy with structural context does not depend on any one system.
Alternative fixed formulas. The priority score combines an energy term and a context term through one soft-saturating function, and that choice is a modeling decision rather than a fitted one. Substituting nine alternative combinations - a hard cap, an exponential, a smooth normalisation, two different energy weightings, a common per-axis anchor, a context term built without the interface bonus, an interface-only context term, and a cohort-standardised combination - gives ROC AUC between 0.878 and 0.947. Eight of the nine keep a graded context term, and all eight rank the cohort almost identically to the reported score (Spearman at least 0.940); their ROC AUC spans 0.878 to 0.947, with the low end belonging to the tier stripped of its interface bonus, which is the ablation reported above rather than a reformulation. The one form that reorders the cohort materially is the interface-only context term, which replaces the graded tier with a binary flag: its ROC AUC is 0.931 but its rank correlation with the reported score falls to 0.782, so what matters is not that it scores slightly lower but that it ranks different variants. None of these was used to select the reported score, and no formula was fitted to this cohort.
Two of the alternatives reach 0.947, marginally above the reported 0.943. The difference is 2 discordant pairs out of the 510 positive-negative pairs the statistic is computed over, which is below the resolution at which two rankings can be distinguished on a cohort of this size. One of the two, the cohort-standardised combination, is additionally not a per-variant score: it z-scores each term against the mean and standard deviation of whichever variants are present, so a variant's value changes when other variants are added or removed, and it cannot be computed for a new variant without a reference cohort. It is retained here only as a comparator. The reported score uses fixed constants and depends on no other variant, which is the property that makes it usable prospectively.

S3 Text. Pathogenicity comparison, robustness, independent validation and a self-caught ground-truth correction.
Section 1. Pathogenicity comparison, directional enrichment, and gain of function
At the 2.5 reference, 27 of 61 benchmark variants had at least one firing energetic output and 34 were silent, where firing uses the shipped reduction over confidence-passing axes rather than a raw threshold on every partner column. Among the 49 variants carrying a curated clinical annotation, 37 were pathogenic. All 22 annotated variants with a firing output were pathogenic, so no benign variant in this cohort produced one, whereas the silent group contained 15 pathogenic and 12 benign variants (Fisher exact p = 2.0 × 10^-4). The 15 pathogenic-but-silent variants spanned 10 of the 14 systems. Because the benchmark was selected for mechanism evidence, these fractions are not expected clinical predictive values; they establish only the direction of the asymmetry between positive and silent structural calls.
Section 2. Robustness, uncertainty, and metric reconciliation
The versioned per-variant mechanism grades and output-level agreement fields are the authoritative source records. In the manuscript, structural agreement is described as a four-output continuity aggregate and is decomposed into the three energetic axes plus tier. The primary convention excludes the four ungraded scope or commitment cases from both whole-variant mechanism scoring and the continuity denominator, giving 92/124 = 0.742 at 2.5 and 90/124 = 0.726 at the prediction-interval bound. The all-row denominator audit is 92/125 = 0.736 at 2.5. Naive recounts based only on raw ΔΔG sign or per-partner values do not reproduce the metric because the shipped scorer applies direction, operating-point, confidence, and interval gates.
Restricting the primary graded set to crystal or confident site models removes the seven partial-site variants. A636P remains in this sensitivity because it is a confident-site case, so its two newly committed neutral fold expectations still count as prediction disagreements. The restricted cohort scored 37.5/50 = 0.750 against 41.0/57 = 0.719 on the full graded set, a difference of 0.031. The confidence restriction therefore removes lower-confidence observations without turning the remaining cohort into a hidden high-performing subset; it should be read as a robustness view, not as an alternative benchmark definition. The two pLDDT thresholds operate together rather than as alternative configurations: a partner interface enters the gated set at 50, confidence is upgraded to high at 70, and the seven interfaces lying between those values are exactly the seven partial-site variants removed here.
Section 3. Independent binding-axis validation on SKEMPI 2.0
The hemoglobin series provides a high-quality within-system anchor but does not test generalization across unrelated interfaces. We therefore scored two independent SKEMPI 2.0 complexes [56] with the frozen COMAVI workflow: barnase-barstar (24 substitutions) and TEM1-BLIP (24 substitutions on the unambiguously mapped BLIP side). Measured binding ΔΔG was calculated as RT ln(K_D,mut/K_D,wt).
Section 4. Do high-priority variants also have their mechanism graded correctly?
The prioritization and mechanism-grading results answer different questions, and reporting them separately leaves open whether they agree: a variant ranked highly but graded inconsistent would point a user at the right variant with the wrong mechanistic hypothesis. Crossing the two over the 47-variant prioritization population answers the question only partly. Of the 17 variants with a modeled structural mechanism, 16 fall inside the top 20 and the one outside, HBB W37Y, ranks 21st and is graded partial. With a single variant beyond the cut there is no population to contrast against: the mean grade is 0.594 inside against 0.500 outside, and the rank-grade association across all 17 is not significant (Spearman rho = 0.356, p = 0.161). The test is underpowered because the ranking leaves almost nothing outside the cut, not because the two outputs disagree. Both grades sit below the 0.719 whole-cohort score because they are computed within the structural class alone, where the mean grade is 0.588; the whole-cohort figure also contains the curated negatives, on which COMAVI scores 0.812. What can be stated without a significance test is the property that matters operationally: no variant graded fully consistent falls outside the top 20, so the ranking does not bury a variant whose mechanism COMAVI resolves. Within the top 20 the grades are 5 consistent, 9 partial and 2 inconsistent; the two inconsistent cases are MLH1 R755W at rank 11 and PIK3CA E545K at rank 15, and they are the failure mode this analysis is designed to expose - correctly prioritized, incorrectly explained. Only one structural mechanism falls outside the top 20: HBB W37Y at rank 21, immediately below the cut, graded partial. Because a single variant sits beyond the cut, the inside-versus-outside contrast cannot be tested and the descriptive values should not be read as an association; what the crossing does establish is that no fully resolved mechanism is ranked below the cut. This is consistent rather than compounding.
Section 5. A correction the benchmark caught in itself
Every committed expectation in this benchmark carries a written one-line evidence basis and a primary citation alongside its expected token. A shipped gate, scripts/verify_ledger_token_basis.py, reads each basis, keeps the clauses that carry the vocabulary of the axis being committed, and fails if the token asserts disruption where those clauses assert the axis is intact, or the reverse. It is run in continuous integration with the other audits.
Three of the 98 committed expectations failed that check, and all three were binding-axis tokens introduced by a single earlier curation review that changed them from neutral to destabilizing without changing the bases beside them. MSH2 C697F carried a destabilizing binding expectation against a basis reading "ATPase catalytic defect, protein stable, interaction intact". KRAS G12D and G12V carried destabilizing binding expectations against bases recording WT-like RAF1 binding with impaired GTP hydrolysis, and noting that residue 12 lies 13.6 A from the RAF1 interface. In each case the lesion is catalytic and the binding axis is not the axis it affects. The three tokens were returned to neutral and the three variants are now curated as carrying no lesion on the modeled axes.
The correction is reported rather than absorbed because its effects run in both directions. KRAS G12D and G12V move from graded inconsistent to graded consistent, and they are no longer counted among the variants the priority score fails to rank, which removes a limitation the earlier analysis stated. MSH2 C697F moves the other way: it is now a curated negative on which COMAVI predicts 13.3 kcal/mol of isolated-subunit destabilization at a buried-core residue, so it becomes a false positive, it lowers the rejection arm, and it is the one non-structural variant in the highest priority-score band. A correction that only improved the result would deserve more suspicion than this one.
The gate cannot detect every curation error. It reads one curated sentence per axis, so it catches contradictions that are written down; it cannot catch an expectation whose basis is silent on the axis, whose citation is wrong, or which is simply mistaken in a way the curator also wrote down. A passing gate means no committed token contradicts its own recorded evidence, which is weaker than every token being correct.
