COMAVI: complex-aware structural mechanism profiles for prioritizing missense variants
Abstract
Researchers often identify more missense variants than they can characterize experimentally. Pathogenicity scores can rank variants that may matter, but they generally do not identify the structural process affected or the experiment most likely to test it. COMAVI (Complex-Aware Variant Impact Scoring) evaluates each variant in an isolated protein and, when available, in the assembled complex. It reports predicted changes in isolated-subunit stability, stability calculated in the assembled complex, and protein-protein interaction energy, together with a structural-context tier. From the same evidence, COMAVI produces two linked outputs. The priority score ranks the strength of modeled structural-disruption evidence; the mechanism profile preserves the signed evidence needed to propose a lesion and select an experiment. Neither output is a pathogenicity verdict.
The 61-variant literature-curated resource supported two overlapping analyses across 14 protein systems. Mechanism-pattern recovery was evaluated in 57 variants: 47 interaction variants and 10 monomer-only BRCA1 tandem-BRCT variants. At the 2.5 kcal/mol reference threshold, COMAVI achieved a partial-credit mechanism-pattern score of 39.5/57 (0.693). Structural prioritization was evaluated in the overlapping set of 47 interaction variants with a committed structural endpoint; the priority score placed 17 of 20 variants with modeled structural mechanisms in the top 20. Those 17 also carry the higher mechanism-pattern grades: mean grade 0.588 inside the top 20 against 0.167 outside it, with no fully consistent variant ranked below the cut (S9 Text). These results evaluate the two intended uses of COMAVI: recovering the expected combination of structural effects and concentrating structurally disruptive variants near the top of a candidate list.
The energy threshold used to convert each continuous predicted ΔΔG into a yes-or-no mechanism call changed the balance between recovery and conservatism. Lower thresholds recovered more candidate mechanisms, whereas higher thresholds produced fewer structural assertions and missed more experimentally measured destabilizations. FoldX generally preserved the ordering of measured effects but compressed large magnitudes, explaining why stringent thresholds reduced sensitivity.
COMAVI is therefore best used in two stages. First, use the priority score to allocate experimental attention. Second, inspect the signed mechanism profile, structural context, uncertainty, selected threshold, and model limitations to choose a stability, assembly, or binding experiment. A silent result means only that no lesion was detected in the supplied static model at the stated threshold; it does not exclude pathogenicity or a mechanism outside that model.
1. Introduction
Sequencing studies often identify more missense candidates than can be characterized experimentally. Researchers must therefore decide not only which variants may matter, but also which variants justify wet-lab investment and which experiment should be performed first. A rank alone cannot distinguish a stability assay from an affinity measurement, an assembly experiment, or a search for a dynamic or regulatory defect.
Variant-effect predictors answer a related but different question. Sequence- and evolution-based models estimate whether a substitution is likely to be damaging; AlphaMissense, for example, provides proteome-wide pathogenicity scores [1]. A pathogenicity score alone generally does not specify which structural process is affected or which experiment should test it. Similar scores can describe variants that destabilize a folded domain, weaken an interface, perturb an assembled subunit, alter catalysis, or change dynamics. Those possibilities require different experiments.
Many proteins function in complexes, so the structural context of a residue can change when partners assemble. A site exposed in an isolated chain may become buried in a complex; a substitution tolerated in the isolated subunit may destabilize the assembled model or weaken one partner interaction. Conversely, an interface substitution can change affinity while leaving the isolated fold intact. Experiments that measure folding and binding separately reveal distinct energetic and allosteric effects [2]. A method intended to guide follow-up should preserve this monomer-versus-multimer distinction rather than collapse it into one impact score.
Large-scale structural annotation tools now place variants in experimental and predicted structures, and some frameworks can analyze both individual proteins and complexes [3-10]. Explicitly comparing the same substitution in isolated and assembled coordinates, however, remains uncommon. MAVISp is a broad modular framework that includes stability and local-interaction analyses across supplied structural contexts [4]. COMAVI addresses the narrower experimental-triage question: it directly contrasts isolated and assembled calculations for the same variant, keeps fold and binding predictions separate, records what the supplied model can and cannot represent, and links the resulting profile to an experimental next step. Related stability and interface methods provide complementary benchmarks and predictors [11-18].
We developed COMAVI to compare a variant across isolated and assembled structural contexts and to translate the resulting evidence into an experimental hypothesis. We then asked whether the mechanism profiles recovered literature-supported structural effects and whether a fixed priority score could concentrate variants with modeled structural lesions near the top of a candidate list. The intended output is assay-directed structural triage, not clinical classification.
2. Materials and Methods
2.1 What COMAVI computes
COMAVI takes a missense variant plus isolated and assembled structural models and calculates three FoldX 5.1 energy changes: stability of the isolated subunit, stability calculated in the assembled complex, and interaction energy for each protein partner. Here, "stability calculated in the assembled complex" (formally, complex-context stability) means the FoldX BuildModel mutation-induced folding-energy change computed using the assembled coordinates. The term identifies the structural context of the calculation; it does not describe a folding pathway or prove that a subunit folds normally in isolation and becomes destabilized only after binding.
From these energies and an energy-free Tier 1-4 summary of local structural context, COMAVI returns two linked outputs (Fig 1). The priority score (ISDS-v1) combines energy magnitude and structural context to rank variants for follow-up. The mechanism profile retains the signed energy changes, tier, uncertainty, selected threshold, and model limitations needed to choose an experiment. Because the three energy changes can occur together, COMAVI does not force a variant into one mechanism label: a profile may support an isolated-fold effect, an assembled-complex effect, a binding effect, more than one of these, or no detected effect at the selected threshold. Before a silent result is interpreted, the supplied model must also be checked for the partner, ligand, state, or modification required for the relevant biology.
Fig 1. COMAVI converts structural calculations into two complementary products. The three energy calculations - isolated-subunit stability, stability calculated in the assembled complex, and partner-specific binding energy - contribute to both products: they supply the energy component of the priority score and the signed values of the mechanism profile. The structural-context tier contributes only to the priority score, as its context component. The mechanism profile is therefore read from the energies alone, which is what keeps a strong structural context from being reported as a mechanism. The priority score ranks variants for structural follow-up. The mechanism profile preserves signed energies, thresholded calls, uncertainty, context, and model limitations to choose an experiment. Pathogenicity remains a separate evidence dimension.
2.2 Benchmark populations and literature-defined expectations
The 61-variant literature-curated resource spans 14 protein systems: 49 interaction variants across 13 complexes and 12 BRCA1 tandem-BRCT variants with measured isolated-subunit stability [19]. The resource supported several overlapping analyses. Whole-variant mechanism-pattern recovery was evaluated in 57 variants: 47 interaction variants and 10 BRCT core or fold variants. Partner-dependent outputs do not apply to the monomer-only BRCT panel and were omitted rather than scored as negative. Four variants were excluded from whole-variant grading because the relevant mechanism was not represented by the supplied structure or was not sufficiently established in the literature. Table 1 gives the composition of the resource by system, and S1 Table lists every variant with its clinical annotation, its expected mechanism, and the published result that defined that expectation. That table cites the defining publication per variant; the primary literature underlying the curated expectations across the 14 systems is [20-38].
Structural prioritization was evaluated in the overlapping set of 47 interaction variants that had both a structural-context tier and a committed endpoint: 20 with a modeled structural mechanism and 27 curated to lack a lesion on the modeled COMAVI axes. Pathogenicity comparisons used clinically labelled complete-case subsets and therefore answer a different question. The population, positive definition, and denominator are stated for each analysis and figure.
Table 1. Benchmark composition. The same prespecified cohort of 61 missense variants across 14 protein systems is used for every analysis in this paper. Mechanism-gradeable counts the variants carrying a committed expected mechanism on at least one applicable axis; the monomer-only BRCA1 BRCT panel contributes no partner-dependent axis and therefore no structural-context tier. Per-variant detail, including the literature evidence that defined each expectation, is given in S1 Table.
Primary literature defined the expected direction of each applicable energy calculation before COMAVI results were considered. Evidence was classified as quantitative energetic, quantitative functional, qualitative experimental, population-supported, or inferred, and by how directly it supported the structural effect. An inferred expectation was used when published mechanistic or retained-function evidence supported a specific modeled axis state but no axis-specific assay was available; the absence of a dedicated assay alone was not treated as evidence. Unknown or inapplicable axes were omitted rather than treated as negative. S2 Table gives the definitions and counts.
Because disruptive variants are more likely than neutral controls to receive dedicated biophysical measurements, all evidence categories were retained in the main benchmark. We then repeated the tier analysis in stricter subsets. Requiring E1-E3 support for every committed axis retained n = 26 and gave sensitivity 0.875 and specificity 0.600; requiring direct-or-coupled support retained the same 26 variants and gave the same values. These were sensitivity analyses, not alternative benchmark definitions, and they did not fit evidence weights or retune tier boundaries. Population-supported and inferred neutral expectations were reported as such, not as measured neutrality.
2.2.1 Model scope: can the supplied structure represent the mechanism?
Before scoring a known mechanism, we asked whether the supplied structure contained the biological context needed for that mechanism to appear: the relevant partner, ligand, modification, oligomeric state, and conformation. This model-scope decision was made independently of COMAVI predictions. We assessed technical usability - residue mapping, structural confidence, and uncertainty - separately. For new variants without established mechanisms, the public pipeline reports the calculations and their technical usability but does not infer a literature-grounded mechanism or model-scope class. S3 Table gives the framework.
When the required context was absent, a silent COMAVI result could not test the known mechanism. The isolated BRCA1 BRCT structure, for example, represents fold energetics but not phosphopeptide recognition by R1699L/Q. VHL Y98H lacks the relevant HIF-bound context, HBB E6V requires the deoxygenated polymerization state, and CALM1 N98S depends strongly on calcium-linked dynamics. These cases were treated as limitations of the supplied model rather than as negative structural evidence.
2.3 Structures and energy calculations
COMAVI scores a variant only in the context of a supplied multimeric model, so it applies to complexes whose composition and interaction partners are already known; it does not discover partners, and a mechanism that acts through a state absent from the supplied model is outside its scope rather than scored as absent. S3 Table states the eligibility rules and the mechanisms they exclude. Complex structures were predicted with the AlphaFold 3 server [39]. Experimental structures replaced predicted models when the relevant site or interface lacked adequate confidence: 2HHB for hemoglobin, 6XI7 for KRAS-RAF1, and 1JM7 for BRCA1-BARD1. The BRCA1 tandem-BRCT monomer panel used 1JNX. All energy calculations used FoldX 5.1 [40] with default energy-function parameters. Each structure was repaired once with RepairPDB and reused across variants.
For each variant, BuildModel was run five times on the isolated subunit and on the assembled model. ΔΔG is reported as mutant minus wild type in kcal/mol, so positive values are destabilizing. The mean across replicates is the axis value, and the replicate standard deviation is retained. AnalyseComplex was then applied to each FoldX-generated mutant complex to calculate interaction-energy changes for each partner. Summary outputs use the most disruptive partner, while partner-level values remain available. S1 Text provides the complete commands and implementation details.
BuildModel and AnalyseComplex answer different questions. BuildModel estimates folding-energy changes for the chains and coordinates supplied to it; AnalyseComplex estimates changes in inter-chain interaction energy. Calculating both prevents an interface-localized fold signal from being mislabeled as a binding-energy change and permits combined fold-plus-binding profiles when both axes fire.
2.4 Structural confidence and usable outputs
FoldX calculates ΔΔG from the supplied coordinates; pLDDT does not numerically rescale those energies. Instead, pLDDT determines whether an energetic axis from a predicted structure can support a mechanism call or enter the priority-score energy component, and it attenuates the structural-context tier. Sites with pLDDT ≥ 70 provide primary confidence. Values from 50 to <70 are retained as lower-confidence evidence when axis-specific uncertainty checks also pass. Below 50, the predicted-structure axis is not used as mechanism evidence, and that partner does not contribute contacts, interface occupancy, or burial. For the tier, the context score is multiplied by 0.7 for best-site pLDDT 50 to <70 and by 0.4 below 50; experimental structures bypass this attenuation. Monomer-fold output is not graded in regions without an independently stable monomeric fold.
2.5 Structural-context tier and priority score
The structural-context tier describes how strongly a site is embedded in features that may make it sensitive to substitution; it contains no FoldX energy and is not itself a mechanism call. It combines substitution severity, local contacts, interface occupancy, burial, and model confidence, then maps the fixed total to Tier 1 (strongest context) through Tier 4 (weakest). S1 Text gives the exact weights and cutoffs.
Because interface occupancy contributes directly to the tier, we tested how much it drove the result. On the same 47-variant prioritization subset, we compared the full tier with interface status alone and with the tier recalculated without its interface bonus. S5 Text, S3 Fig, and the within-system tests report these comparisons.
The same energy and context information produces the integrated structural-disruption score (ISDS-v1), hereafter the priority score. The formula is fixed: it uses the same reference values and tier mapping for every variant set rather than fitting coefficients or standardizing within the current batch. The score ranks the strength of modeled structural-disruption evidence. It is not a pathogenicity probability, does not identify the affected structural process, and has no validated binary cutoff.
For each usable energetic calculation j, COMAVI divides |ΔΔG_j| by a fixed reference value τ_j: 2.9 kcal/mol for isolated-subunit and assembled-complex stability and 3.5 kcal/mol for binding. The largest ratio, R, represents the strongest reliable energetic signal without adding correlated isolated and assembled stability effects.
R = max_j (|ΔΔG_j| / τ_j)
COMAVI softly compresses R so that one extreme FoldX prediction cannot dominate the ranking.
E = R / (1 + R)
The tier number T is converted to a context component C, mapping Tiers 1-4 to 1.000, 0.667, 0.333, and 0.
C = (4 - T) / 3
ISDS-v1 = (E + C) / 2
The final priority score gives equal weight to energy magnitude and structural context. Only confidence-passing energy calculations enter R, while the signed values and all available axes remain visible in the mechanism profile. All four tiers contribute continuously; the separate Tier 1-2 cutoff is used only in descriptive comparator analyses and is not part of ISDS-v1. Because the score does not use the threshold that converts a continuous energy into a mechanism call, it is unchanged when that threshold changes. A value above 0.5 is not a validated positive call.
2.6 Energy thresholds and descriptive evidence states
A continuous predicted energy becomes a yes-or-no mechanism call only after an energy threshold is chosen. The threshold is the minimum absolute ΔΔG required to call that axis positive at a given operating point. We evaluated uniform thresholds of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, and 2.5 kcal/mol, plus the more conservative per-axis tSAP setting: 2.9 kcal/mol for isolated-subunit stability, 2.9 for assembled-complex stability, and 3.5 for binding [41]. The released software uses 1.0 kcal/mol as a screening-oriented default. We use 2.5 kcal/mol as the frozen benchmark reference and tSAP as the most conservative setting evaluated. None is universally optimal.
Lower thresholds call more structural effects and are useful when missing a candidate mechanism is costly. Higher thresholds make fewer calls and are useful when false mechanistic assertions are more costly. Every report should therefore show both the continuous ΔΔG and the threshold used to call it.
We also combined energy calls and tier into four descriptive states - both positive, energy only, context only, or neither - to summarize evidence patterns without redefining the physical mechanism. Definitions and analyses are in S5 Text; these states are not a validated classifier.
2.7 Benchmark outcomes and statistical analysis
For each variant, we asked whether COMAVI recovered the expected combination of applicable structural effects. The reader-facing mechanism-pattern score is the mean of a three-level partial-credit grade: 1 for a fully consistent whole-variant pattern, 0.5 for a partly correct multi-axis pattern, and 0 for an inconsistent pattern. This outcome is formally weighted mechanism consistency; it is not ordinary exact-match accuracy. Unknown or inapplicable effects were omitted. Table 2 gives the full rubric.
Table 2. Whole-variant mechanism-pattern grading rubric.
To identify which calculations contributed, we also counted direction agreement separately for isolated-subunit stability, assembled-complex stability, and binding. Predicted-versus-measured analyses assessed rank correlation, scale compression, and the number of experimentally destabilizing effects recovered at each threshold. Continuity metrics and denominator audits are reported in S7 Text.
For structural prioritization, the positive class was the 20 variants with a modeled structural mechanism and the negative class was the 27 no-lesion variants in the 47-variant subset. Receiver operating characteristic area under the curve (ROC AUC) summarizes ordering across the full list: it is the probability that a randomly selected structural-mechanism variant ranks above a randomly selected no-lesion variant. Average precision summarizes precision across the recall curve. Top-k recovery asks how many of the 20 structural mechanisms would be encountered if a researcher could test only the first 10, 20, or 25 variants. Formula comparisons, score bands, and component analyses were treated as internal sensitivity analyses because the same resource informed score development.
Variants from the same protein system share structures and curation, so the main uncertainty analyses resampled whole systems rather than treating every variant as independent. We also examined leave-one-system-out results, variant-level bootstrap intervals, label permutations, and replicate noise. S1 Text lists the fixed seeds and draw counts.
2.8 Software, code, and data availability
FoldX 5.1 was used for energy calculations; Python with pandas, scipy, and scikit-learn was used for scoring and statistics. Code, reference outputs, figures, and regression tests are available at https://github.com/la424/comavi. The priority score is implemented once in the shared COMAVI engine, so benchmark, congenital heart disease (CHD), generic, and live or external runs receive the same versioned fields. FoldX and large structural-input collections are not redistributed.
3. Results
3.1 COMAVI compares isolated and assembled contexts to propose experiments
COMAVI is designed to compare the same variant across structural contexts. It evaluates the isolated subunit and, when an assembly is available, the assembled complex, then separates predicted changes in subunit stability from changes in partner interaction. The priority score ranks candidates, whereas the mechanism profile retains the signed evidence needed to choose an experiment. Because the three energy changes are evaluated separately, one variant can support more than one structural hypothesis.
CFH R78G illustrates a binding-specific result: its predicted isolated-subunit change was modest (0.93 kcal/mol), whereas predicted binding disruption was 6.12 kcal/mol, consistent with an increase in the measured dissociation constant from approximately 12 μM to more than 35 μM [42]. VWF R1334Q shows the value of evaluating the assembled complex. Its isolated-subunit prediction was nearly neutral (0.01 kcal/mol), but assembled-complex stability and binding changed by 4.73 and 4.11 kcal/mol [43]. The profile therefore proposes a combined assembly-and-binding lesion rather than an isolated-fold lesion.
A silent result requires equal care, and it is not a benign call: COMAVI reports whether a modeled lesion was detected on the axes it can evaluate, and never returns a pathogenicity verdict. Two different situations produce silence, and they carry different meaning. The first is an in-scope variant with no lesion above threshold on any applicable axis, which is an informative negative for the mechanisms modeled. The second is a mechanism that acts through a state the supplied structure does not contain, where silence is uninformative rather than negative. HBB E6V is the clearest example of the second: it acts through deoxygenated hemoglobin polymerization, a state absent from the supplied model, so COMAVI silence there cannot test the known mechanism and instead redirects follow-up toward the missing assembly state. BRCA1 R1699L/Q and CALM1 N98S are related, involving phosphopeptide recognition and calcium-dependent dynamics. The eligibility screen in S3 Table is what separates the two situations, and it is applied before scoring rather than after.
3.2 COMAVI recovers expected mechanism patterns across diverse systems
This and every subsequent analysis use the same prespecified cohort of 61 variants across 14 protein systems described in Section 2.2 and listed in Table 1 and S1 Table; no variant was added or removed after scoring began. At the 2.5 kcal/mol reference threshold, COMAVI recovered the expected combination of structural effects with a mechanism-pattern score of 39.5/57 = 0.693. This partial-credit result represents 39.5 total points across 57 graded variants, not 39.5 exact matches. Because the rubric awards partial credit and because half the graded cohort carries a curated expectation of no structural lesion, the aggregate lies between two quantities that are more directly interpretable. Among the 28 variants with a committed structural mechanism, at least one of the three axes exceeded the reference threshold for 21 (0.750), and the axis to which the mechanism is attributed in the literature exceeded it for 14 (0.500); among the 29 variants curated to lack a lesion on the modeled axes, no axis exceeded it for 24 (0.828). COMAVI therefore detects structural disruption more reliably than it localizes the disrupted axis, and the gap between those two rates is the method's principal current limitation. S3 Text reports the decomposition by expected mechanism class. The interaction and BRCT subsets had similar scores, so neither cohort determined the pooled result. Because the data span 14 clustered systems, 0.693 is benchmark-specific pattern agreement rather than universal accuracy.
At the output level, 56 of 77 applicable energetic calculations had the expected direction: 19/25 for isolated-subunit stability, 17/22 for stability calculated in the assembled complex, and 20/30 for binding. Fig 2 shows these axis-specific counts and the whole-variant partial-credit score. The per-axis rates identify which calculations contributed, but they do not replace the whole-variant outcome.

Evidence-restricted analyses did not change the qualitative conclusion. Among variants for which every committed axis had E1-E3 support, tier sensitivity was 0.875 and specificity 0.600 (n = 26); the direct-or-coupled subset returned the same values on the same 26 variants. Because evidence class was strongly tied to whether an axis was expected to be neutral or destabilizing, these analyses were treated as sensitivity views rather than alternative primary benchmarks (S2 Text).
No single system carried the result. Resampling entire protein systems gave a 95% interval of 0.604-0.790 for the mechanism-pattern score, and leave-one-system-out values ranged from 0.676 to 0.713. Permutation, replicate-noise, and continuity audits are reported in S7 Text.
3.3 The energy threshold controls recovery and false structural calls
The energy threshold determines the balance between missed mechanisms and false structural calls. Across the prespecified menu the two error types move in opposite directions and by unequal amounts. Of the 28 variants with a committed structural mechanism, the number missed rises from 10 at 1.0 kcal/mol to 12 at the 2.5 kcal/mol reference and 16 at the substitution-adjusted threshold; of the 29 variants curated to lack a modeled lesion, the number given a false structural call falls from 15 to 5 and then 4 across the same range. Raising the threshold from 1.0 to 2.5 therefore removes ten false calls at a cost of two missed mechanisms, while the final step to the substitution-adjusted threshold removes one more false call at a cost of four. The same tradeoff appeared in measured-energy comparisons. Of 44 experimentally destabilizing effects, 35 were detected at the 1.0 kcal/mol default, 13 at the 2.5 kcal/mol reference, and 12 at tSAP. Thus a conservative threshold can miss a real effect even when the measured destabilization is large.
FoldX preserved ordering better than magnitude. Across the broader 56-point calibration set, predicted and measured effects had Spearman ρ = 0.63, but the fitted slope was 0.43 and error became more negative as measured effects increased. Large destabilizations were therefore systematically compressed, which explains part of the sensitivity loss at high thresholds.

The present benchmark contains no direct measured energy comparator for the assembled-complex stability calculation, so that axis is evaluated against literature-defined direction and mechanism expectations rather than energy calibration. Together, these results support reporting the direction and rank of predicted energy, its continuous value, replicate uncertainty, and the selected threshold. They do not justify treating a FoldX ΔΔG as a measured energy or converting it to experimental units. The priority score remains continuous when the call threshold changes, so ranking and mechanism calling remain separate decisions.
3.4 Structural context and energy support practical prioritization
The structural-context tier identified 18 of 20 variants with modeled structural mechanisms as Tier 1 or 2, but it also placed 12 of 27 no-lesion variants in those tiers. It was therefore sensitive but not specific. Interface status alone was more specific, whereas the full tier recovered three structural variants outside a detected interface. The exact comparator table and within-system tests are in S4 Table and S3 Fig.
The tier describes whether the mutated site lies in a structurally susceptible environment. It does not identify which energetic process is affected and should not override an axis-specific energy prediction. Its components therefore remain visible, and its fixed cut-points require prospective testing.
We next asked whether combining energy magnitude and structural context moved variants with modeled structural lesions toward the top of a candidate list. This analysis used the overlapping set of 47 interaction variants with a committed structural endpoint: 20 structural-mechanism variants and 27 variants curated to lack a lesion on the modeled axes. The fixed priority score is defined in Methods.
The priority score placed 17 of 20 structural-mechanism variants in the top 20 and 18 of 20 in the top 25. Across the complete 47-variant ranking, structural-prioritization ROC AUC was 0.917 and average precision was 0.923. These are internal ranking results, not external performance estimates, pathogenicity estimates, or calibrated probabilities.

3.5 COMAVI complements pathogenicity prediction and directs follow-up experiments
Pathogenicity prediction, structural prioritization, and mechanism interpretation answer different questions. On the common clinically labelled set of 47 variants, AlphaMissense was the stronger pathogenicity ranker (ROC AUC 0.903); the priority score had pathogenicity ROC AUC 0.851, its energy component 0.836, and its context component 0.769. These pathogenicity AUCs should not be compared directly with the priority score's structural-prioritization ROC AUC of 0.917 because the positive definition and task differ. AlphaMissense asks whether a variant may be pathogenic; the priority score asks how strongly the available structure supports modeled disruption; the mechanism profile identifies the proposed structural process and experiment.
Discordant cases show why all three views are useful. HBB E6V can be pathogenic despite a low structural score because its polymerization mechanism is absent from the supplied state. CFH R78G and VWF R1334Q show the converse: multimer-aware profiles identify binding-specific or combined assembly-and-binding hypotheses that a pathogenicity score cannot name. PIK3CA H1047R and TNNI3 R162W run the other way again: both sit in a strong structural context, yet neither produces an energetic lesion above threshold on any modeled axis. COMAVI therefore ranks them for follow-up on context alone and declines to name a mechanism, which is the intended behaviour rather than a missed call - a high priority score is a claim about where to look, not a claim that a specific axis was disrupted.

COMAVI scores remain structural descriptors, and the mechanism profile provides the process-specific interpretation.
Silent structural results occurred in both pathogenic and benign variants in this selected benchmark (S6 Text), so silence does not distinguish the two. That observation is the reason the transferable conclusion is asymmetric: a positive structural call supplies a testable hypothesis, whereas a silent call provides little evidence against pathogenicity, because the mechanism may simply lie outside what the supplied model can represent.
A practical COMAVI workflow has two steps. First, use the priority score to decide which variants deserve structural follow-up. Second, inspect the signed isolated-subunit, assembled-complex, and binding predictions, together with uncertainty, threshold, tier components, and model limitations, to choose the experiment. A high score without a thresholded energy call remains context-based prioritization rather than a named lesion.
4. Discussion
4.1 Two linked outputs answer two practical questions
COMAVI helps answer two practical questions: which variants deserve structural follow-up, and which experiment should test the predicted effect. At the 2.5 kcal/mol reference threshold, the mechanism profile recovered the expected structural pattern with a partial-credit score of 39.5/57 = 0.693. In the overlapping 47-variant prioritization subset, the priority score placed 17 of 20 modeled structural mechanisms within the top 20 candidates. These results support mechanism-directed triage and prioritization within the present literature-curated resource; they do not establish universal accuracy, clinical validity, or external generalization.
COMAVI's main scientific advantage is that it evaluates the same substitution in an isolated subunit and in the assembled complex. This comparison can distinguish a predicted folding problem from a predicted partner-binding problem or identify both. CFH R78G and VWF R1334Q show how that decomposition maps to affinity or assembly experiments, whereas HBB E6V shows why a missing structural state must limit interpretation.
The priority score provides a transparent way to allocate limited experimental attention, but it cannot identify the affected process. The mechanism profile remains necessary for interpretation. The tier describes site context; it does not select a FoldX axis or override an axis-specific energy result. Prospective testing on new protein systems is therefore essential.
4.2 How to use COMAVI: rank, interpret, and test
Threshold choice should follow the cost of error. The 1.0 kcal/mol default is screening-oriented; 1.5-2.0 provides intermediate stringency; 2.5 is the frozen benchmark reference; and tSAP is the most conservative setting evaluated. Lower thresholds are preferable when missing a candidate mechanism is more costly, whereas higher thresholds are preferable when false structural assertions are more costly.
Every call should retain its continuous energy and threshold because near-boundary variants lose information when reduced to a binary label. A silent result must not be translated into benign, nonfunctional, or unworthy of study. It means only that the supplied static model did not show a structural lesion at the selected threshold.
COMAVI should be used alongside pathogenicity predictors, population evidence, functional assays, and clinical interpretation frameworks such as Franklin [46]. The practical sequence is simple: use the priority score to allocate attention, then use the signed mechanism profile and model limitations to design the experiment. Pathogenicity evidence remains a separate question. Reporting a pathogenicity score alongside the COMAVI outputs would be straightforward to implement, and the comparison in Section 3.5 shows what such a display would and would not add: the two disagree in interpretable ways, so showing them together is useful provided they are not combined into a single score, which would reintroduce the conflation the separation is designed to avoid.
4.3 Limitations and validation priorities
FoldX predictions preserve direction and rank better than absolute magnitude. Large destabilizations are compressed, so stringent thresholds can miss real effects and the priority score cannot be interpreted in experimental energy units. The score's compression of extreme predictions limits their influence but does not calibrate FoldX.
The benchmark is small and clustered: 57 mechanism-evaluable variants span 14 systems, and variants within a system share structures and curation. Whole-system resampling addresses that dependence but cannot replace prospective validation. The priority-score formula and its initial evaluation used the same literature-curated resource, so the reported structural-prioritization ROC AUC and average precision are internal results that require testing without retuning. Evidence quality is also uneven: isolated-subunit stability and binding have direct energy anchors, whereas the present benchmark contains no direct measured energy comparator for stability calculated in the assembled complex.
Finally, COMAVI evaluates the supplied static coordinates. It cannot capture every catalytic, dynamic, aggregative, regulatory, post-translational, ligand-dependent, or state-dependent mechanism. Model limitations must therefore be assessed before a silent result is interpreted. Versioned literature curation remains important, and detailed evidence updates are documented in the supplement and repository.
The highest-value next tests are external panels that separate folding from binding and include both disruptive and retained-function variants. Additional VWF and CFH variants can strengthen validation of fold-neutral binding lesions; SOD1, transthyretin, and dense PDZ, SH3, and GB1 landscapes can test coupled tertiary, quaternary, interface, and allosteric effects [2,47,48]. The priority-score formula is now frozen and should be evaluated on these systems without retuning. The current priority analysis is restricted to interaction variants because its structural-context tier was defined and evaluated in that setting. Extending a comparable tier and priority score to monomer-only systems would require a prespecified formula and prospective validation rather than reusing the current cut-points by assumption.
4.4 Conclusion
COMAVI converts a list of missense variants into two linked outputs: a fixed priority for structural follow-up and a mechanism profile that identifies the proposed structural process and experimental test. In this benchmark, it recovered useful mechanism patterns and concentrated modeled structural mechanisms near the top of the ranking. Its value lies in directing experiments, not replacing pathogenicity prediction. Responsible use requires the priority score, signed profile, selected threshold, structural context, and model limitations to be interpreted together.

S1 Text - Implementation details and statistical conventions
Each structural context was repaired once and cached. Variants were specified in FoldX notation. BuildModel was run five times on both isolated and assembled coordinates, and AnalyseComplex was applied pairwise to each resulting mutant complex. Beyond the command, input, replicate count, partner chains, and output directory, no FoldX energy-function parameter was overridden.
foldx --command=RepairPDB --pdb=<structure> --output-dir=<workdir>
foldx --command=BuildModel --pdb=<repaired> --mutant-file=individual_list.txt --numberOfRuns=5 --output-dir=<workdir>
foldx --command=AnalyseComplex --pdb=<mutant_complex> --analyseComplexChains=<chainA>,<chainB> --output-dir=<workdir>
The mutant-minus-wild-type ΔΔG was read from FoldX outputs. Wild-type interaction energy was computed once per partner pair, and mutant interaction energy was computed on each of the five BuildModel replicates. Partner-level binding ΔΔG is the mean of IE(mutant) - IE(wild type); replicate standard deviation is retained.
Structural-context tier implementation. The raw score is Grantham substitution severity [49] multiplied by the sum of monomer contacts and the maximum confidence-passing inter-chain contact count, plus an interface bonus (1.5 for one partner; 2.0 for at least two) and a burial bonus (1.0 for partial burial; 2.0 for a buried core position). The result is multiplied by 1.0 for pLDDT at least 70, 0.7 for pLDDT 50-70, and 0.4 below 50; experimental structures bypass this attenuation. Fixed cutoffs assign Tier 1 at s at least 5.0, Tier 2 at 3.0 to less than 5.0, Tier 3 at 1.5 to less than 3.0, and Tier 4 below 1.5.

Systems, not variants, are the primary resampling unit because variants within one system share a structure, partner set, and curation process. The priority-score analysis uses 10,000 whole-system bootstrap draws (seed 20260821) for ROC AUC, average precision, and paired AUC differences. Tier associations also use Fisher exact tests, exact within-system permutation, and leave-one-system-out diagnostics. The priority score is calculated after energetic confidence and structural-context tier assessment and before threshold-specific mechanism classification. The shared engine writes nine versioned fields: ISDS-v1, energy and context components, the uncapped energy ratio, dominant axis and partner, dominant signed ΔΔG, an availability flag, and the formula version. The locked analysis plan, formula constants, missing-data rules, random seed, bootstrap count, and sensitivity transformations are distributed as YAML and JSON with checksums. Unit tests cover reference values, tier mapping, batch invariance, monotonicity, stabilization, partner selection, confidence gating, and missing inputs.
S2 Text - Evidence provenance and denominator sensitivity
The evidence ledger contains 98 committed energetic-axis expectations. E1-E3 expectations are supported by energetic, functional, or qualitative experiments. E4 denotes population-supported neutral expectations. E5 denotes an inferred axis state used when the literature supported a mechanistic or retained-function interpretation but no axis-specific assay was available; inference was not assigned from missing data alone. E4 and E5 commitments are heavily enriched for neutral expectations: all 9 E4 axes and all 18 E5 axes are neutral. Complex-context stability is the least independently grounded axis: 14 of its 25 committed axes rest on inferred rather than direct or coupled evidence, and 11 are E5 inferred axis states.
Using the all-row 78-axis convention, energetic-output agreement was 20/32 = 0.625 for E1-E2 evidence and 36/46 = 0.783 for E3-E5 evidence (Fisher p = 0.200). Evidence class was strongly associated with expected axis state: direct measurements were preferentially available for destabilizers, whereas frequency-supported and inferred commitments were predominantly neutral. Within expected-destabilizing axes, agreement was 14/24 for E1-E2 and 4/8 for E3-E5; within expected-neutral axes, the corresponding rates were 6/7 and 32/38. The continuity-corrected Mantel-Haenszel estimate across expected-state strata was odds ratio 1.30 (p = 0.95). Thus the current benchmark does not show that evidence category independently explains energetic-output agreement after expected axis state is considered. Among 25 gradeable E1 energetic axes, 18 agreed (0.720).
Strict variant-level sensitivity analyses used all-axis definitions. Requiring every committed axis to have E1-E3 support left n = 26 (tier sensitivity 0.875, specificity 0.600). Requiring every committed axis to be direct or coupled left the same 26 variants (sensitivity 0.875, specificity 0.600).

Weighted mechanism consistency differed by expected mechanism class: 0.833 for mixed structural expectations (n = 6), 0.828 for curated structurally silent variants (n = 29), 0.550 for the fold-mechanism class (n = 10), and 0.417 for pure interface destabilization (n = 12). S4 Text - Operating-point and calibration diagnostics
The apparent pooled optimum depends on class mixture. The benchmark contains 28 variants with committed structural mechanisms and 29 benchmark negatives (structural fraction 0.491). Reweighting the classes moved the pooled optimum: 2.5 remained optimal only while the structural-class weight was below 0.563, above the benchmark's own structural fraction of 0.491. This analysis supports reporting a threshold menu rather than a universal optimum.
In 10,000 paired whole-system resamples, mechanism recovery decreased from 0.714 (95% CI 0.553-0.821) at 1.0 to 0.500 at tSAP, whereas correct rejection increased from 0.448 at 1.0 to 0.862 (0.733-1.000) at tSAP. The probability that recovery fell, and the probability that rejection rose, were each 1.000. The direction of the curves is forced by thresholding, however; resampling quantifies the size of the exchange, not its existence.
The mechanism-attribution menu also answers a different question from the 3 kcal/mol fold cutoff used for cellular-stability or degradation interpretation in MAVISp [4] and related MLH1 studies [50,51]. A predicted fold change below that cellular-abundance cutoff can still identify the correct structural axis for follow-up. We therefore retain the 3 kcal/mol comparison as endpoint context, not as a basis for selecting a COMAVI operating point.
S7 Table. Complete operating-point sweep. MC is weighted mechanism consistency; balanced MC gives structural and benchmark-negative classes equal weight.
Thirty-six of 57 graded variants retained the same grade across all five operating points; 21 changed. The threshold-sensitive variants occupied an approximately 1-3 kcal/mol prediction band. Within that band, variants with structural ground truth had larger predicted magnitudes than benchmark negatives (Mann-Whitney p = 0.0019). Predictions outside this intermediate range were threshold-stable across the evaluated menu.
Four systems supplied 63 predicted-measured comparisons: 15 benchmark comparators and 48 external SKEMPI binding mutations. Forty-four had measured destabilization of at least 1.0 kcal/mol. Across the 56 non-Glu73 comparisons, signed error became more negative as the measured effect increased (Spearman ρ = -0.70, p = 1.4 × 10^-9), indicating progressively stronger underprediction of large effects.

BRCA1 V1808A is sensitive to the prespecified classification rule because its measured value is 0.10 kcal/mol from the threshold while its predicted value is 1.07 kcal/mol above it. We did not invert the regressions to translate COMAVI thresholds into measured-energy equivalents. The fits regress predicted on measured ΔΔG, so inversion is a reverse prediction. The Fieller instability index was 0.236 for the 15-variant benchmark fit and 0.105 for the pooled fit, and the resulting intervals were too wide for a defensible unit conversion. Direct recovery counts therefore remain the primary physical interpretation.
S5 Text - Structural-context comparators, evidence states, and priority-score sensitivity
The full tier, an interface-only screen, and the tier without its interface bonus were compared on the same 47-variant prioritization subset (S4 Table; S3 Fig). At the 2.5 reference, 27 of 61 benchmark variants had at least one firing energetic output and 34 were silent, where firing uses the shipped reduction over confidence-passing axes rather than a raw threshold on every partner column. S7 Text - Robustness, uncertainty, and metric reconciliation
The versioned per-variant mechanism grades and output-level agreement fields are the authoritative source records. In the manuscript, structural agreement is described as a four-output continuity aggregate and is decomposed into the three energetic axes plus tier. The primary convention excludes the four ungraded scope or commitment cases from both whole-variant mechanism scoring and the continuity denominator, giving 89/124 = 0.718 at 2.5 and 87/124 = 0.702 at tSAP. The all-row denominator audit is 89/125 = 0.712 at 2.5. Naive recounts based only on raw ΔΔG sign or per-partner values do not reproduce the metric because the shipped scorer applies direction, operating-point, confidence, and interval gates.
Restricting the primary graded set to crystal or confident site models removes the seven partial-site variants. A636P remains in this sensitivity because it is a confident-site case, so its two newly committed neutral fold expectations still count as prediction disagreements. The restricted cohort scored 36.0/50 = 0.720 against 39.5/57 = 0.693 on the full graded set, a difference of 0.027. The confidence restriction therefore removes lower-confidence observations without turning the remaining cohort into a hidden high-performing subset; it should be read as a robustness view, not as an alternative benchmark definition. The two pLDDT thresholds operate together rather than as alternative configurations: a partner interface enters the gated set at 50, confidence is upgraded to high at 70, and the seven interfaces lying between those values are exactly the seven partial-site variants removed here.

S9 Text - Do high-priority variants also have their mechanism graded correctly?
The prioritization and mechanism-grading results answer different questions, and reporting them separately leaves open whether they agree: a variant ranked highly but graded inconsistent would point a user at the right variant with the wrong mechanistic hypothesis. Crossing the two over the 47-variant prioritization population shows they agree. Of the 20 variants with a modeled structural mechanism, the 17 recovered in the top 20 have a mean mechanism-pattern grade of 0.588, against 0.167 for the three that rank outside it, and the priority score correlates with the grade across all 20 (Spearman rho = 0.482, p = 0.031). No variant graded fully consistent falls outside the top 20, so the ranking does not bury a variant whose mechanism COMAVI resolves. Within the top 20 the grades are 5 consistent, 10 partial and 2 inconsistent; the two inconsistent cases are MLH1 R755W at rank 11 and PIK3CA E545K at rank 15, and they are the failure mode this analysis is designed to expose - correctly prioritized, incorrectly explained. The three structural mechanisms outside the top 20 are HBB W37Y at rank 21, immediately below the cut, and KRAS G12D and G12V at ranks 33 and 39. The KRAS pair is the limitation already stated in Section 4: both act through nucleotide-state-dependent signalling rather than through an interface lesion an interface-weighted index can score, and both are also the variants graded inconsistent outside the top 20. The same mechanism class that the ranking misses is the one the grading misses, which is consistent rather than compounding.
