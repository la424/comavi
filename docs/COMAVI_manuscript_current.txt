COMAVI: complex-aware structural mechanism profiles for prioritizing missense variants
Abstract
Researchers often identify more missense variants than they can characterize experimentally. Pathogenicity scores can rank variants that may matter, but they generally do not identify the structural process affected or the experiment most likely to test it. COMAVI (Complex-Aware Variant Impact Scoring) evaluates each variant in an isolated protein and, when available, in the assembled complex. It reports predicted changes in isolated-subunit stability, stability calculated in the assembled complex, and protein-protein interaction energy, together with a structural-context tier. From the same evidence, COMAVI produces two linked outputs. The priority score ranks the strength of modeled structural-disruption evidence; the mechanism profile preserves the signed evidence needed to propose a lesion and select an experiment. Neither output is a pathogenicity verdict.
The 61-variant literature-curated resource supported two overlapping analyses across 14 protein systems. Mechanism-pattern recovery was evaluated in 57 variants: 47 interaction variants and 10 monomer-only BRCA1 tandem-BRCT variants. At the 2.5 kcal/mol reference threshold, COMAVI achieved a partial-credit mechanism-pattern score of 41/57 (0.719). Structural prioritization was evaluated in the overlapping set of 47 interaction variants with a committed structural endpoint; the priority score placed 16 of 17 variants with modeled structural mechanisms in the top 20. These results evaluate the two intended uses of COMAVI: recovering the expected combination of structural effects and concentrating structurally disruptive variants near the top of a candidate list.
The energy threshold used to convert each continuous predicted ΔΔG into a yes-or-no mechanism call changed the balance between recovery and conservatism. Lower thresholds recovered more candidate mechanisms, whereas higher thresholds produced fewer structural assertions and missed more experimentally measured destabilizations. FoldX generally preserved the ordering of measured effects but compressed large magnitudes, explaining why stringent thresholds reduced sensitivity.
COMAVI is therefore best used in two stages. First, use the priority score to allocate experimental attention. Second, inspect the signed mechanism profile, structural context, uncertainty, selected threshold, and model limitations to choose a stability, assembly, or binding experiment. A silent result means only that no lesion was detected in the supplied static model at the stated threshold; it does not exclude pathogenicity or a mechanism outside that model.
1. Introduction
Sequencing studies often identify more missense candidates than can be characterized experimentally. Researchers must therefore decide not only which variants may matter, but also which variants justify wet-lab investment and which experiment should be performed first. A rank alone cannot distinguish a stability assay from an affinity measurement, an assembly experiment, or a search for a dynamic or regulatory defect.
Variant-effect predictors answer a related but different question. Sequence- and evolution-based models estimate whether a substitution is likely to be damaging; AlphaMissense, for example, provides proteome-wide pathogenicity scores [3]. A pathogenicity score alone generally does not specify which structural process is affected or which experiment should test it. Similar scores can describe variants that destabilize a folded domain, weaken an interface, perturb an assembled subunit, alter catalysis, or change dynamics. Those possibilities require different experiments.
Many proteins function in complexes, so the structural context of a residue can change when partners assemble. A site exposed in an isolated chain may become buried in a complex; a substitution tolerated in the isolated subunit may destabilize the assembled model or weaken one partner interaction. Conversely, an interface substitution can change affinity while leaving the isolated fold intact. Experiments that measure folding and binding separately reveal distinct energetic and allosteric effects [49]. A method intended to guide follow-up should preserve this monomer-versus-multimer distinction rather than collapse it into one impact score.
Large-scale structural annotation tools now place variants in experimental and predicted structures, and some frameworks can analyze both individual proteins and complexes [35,36,41-46]. Explicitly comparing the same substitution in isolated and assembled coordinates, however, remains uncommon. MAVISp is a broad modular framework that includes stability and local-interaction analyses across supplied structural contexts [36]. COMAVI addresses the narrower experimental-triage question: it directly contrasts isolated and assembled calculations for the same variant, keeps fold and binding predictions separate, records what the supplied model can and cannot represent, and links the resulting profile to an experimental next step. Related stability and interface methods provide complementary benchmarks and predictors [10-13,39,40,47,48].
We developed COMAVI to compare a variant across isolated and assembled structural contexts and to translate the resulting evidence into an experimental hypothesis. We then asked whether the mechanism profiles recovered literature-supported structural effects and whether a fixed priority score could concentrate variants with modeled structural lesions near the top of a candidate list. The intended output is assay-directed structural triage, not clinical classification.
2. Materials and Methods
2.1 What COMAVI computes
COMAVI takes a missense variant plus isolated and assembled structural models and calculates three FoldX 5.1 energy changes: stability of the isolated subunit, stability calculated in the assembled complex, and interaction energy for each protein partner. Here, "stability calculated in the assembled complex" (formally, complex-context stability) means the FoldX BuildModel mutation-induced folding-energy change computed using the assembled coordinates. The term identifies the structural context of the calculation; it does not describe a folding pathway or prove that a subunit folds normally in isolation and becomes destabilized only after binding.
From these energies and an energy-free Tier 1-4 summary of local structural context, COMAVI returns two linked outputs (Figure 1). The priority score (ISDS-v1) combines energy magnitude and structural context to rank variants for follow-up. The mechanism profile retains the signed energy changes, tier, uncertainty, selected threshold, and model limitations needed to choose an experiment. Because the three energy changes can occur together, COMAVI does not force a variant into one mechanism label: a profile may support an isolated-fold effect, an assembled-complex effect, a binding effect, more than one of these, or no detected effect at the selected threshold. Before a silent result is interpreted, the supplied model must also be checked for the partner, ligand, state, or modification required for the relevant biology.

2.2 Benchmark populations and literature-defined expectations
The 61-variant literature-curated resource spans 14 protein systems: 49 interaction variants across 13 complexes and 12 BRCA1 tandem-BRCT variants with measured isolated-subunit stability [6]. The resource supported several overlapping analyses. Whole-variant mechanism-pattern recovery was evaluated in 57 variants: 47 interaction variants and 10 BRCT core or fold variants. Partner-dependent outputs do not apply to the monomer-only BRCT panel and were omitted rather than scored as negative. Four variants were excluded from whole-variant grading because the relevant mechanism was not represented by the supplied structure or was not sufficiently established in the literature.
Structural prioritization was evaluated in the overlapping set of 47 interaction variants that had both a structural-context tier and a committed endpoint: 17 with a modeled structural mechanism and 30 curated to lack a lesion on the modeled COMAVI axes. Pathogenicity comparisons used clinically labelled complete-case subsets and therefore answer a different question. The population, positive definition, and denominator are stated for each analysis and figure.
Primary literature defined the expected direction of each applicable energy calculation before COMAVI results were considered. Evidence was classified as quantitative energetic, quantitative functional, qualitative experimental, population-supported, or inferred, and by how directly it supported the structural effect. An inferred expectation was used when published mechanistic or retained-function evidence supported a specific modeled axis state but no axis-specific assay was available; the absence of a dedicated assay alone was not treated as evidence. Unknown or inapplicable axes were omitted rather than treated as negative. Supplementary Table S2 gives the definitions and counts.
Because disruptive variants are more likely than neutral controls to receive dedicated biophysical measurements, all evidence categories were retained in the main benchmark. We then repeated the tier analysis in stricter subsets. Requiring E1-E3 support for every committed axis retained n = 27 and gave sensitivity 1.000 and specificity 0.600; requiring direct-or-coupled support retained n = 25 and gave sensitivity 1.000 and specificity 0.615. These were sensitivity analyses, not alternative benchmark definitions, and they did not fit evidence weights or retune tier boundaries. Population-supported and inferred neutral expectations were reported as such, not as measured neutrality.
2.2.1 Model scope: can the supplied structure represent the mechanism?
Before scoring a known mechanism, we asked whether the supplied structure contained the biological context needed for that mechanism to appear: the relevant partner, ligand, modification, oligomeric state, and conformation. This model-scope decision was made independently of COMAVI predictions. We assessed technical usability - residue mapping, structural confidence, and uncertainty - separately. For new variants without established mechanisms, the public pipeline reports the calculations and their technical usability but does not infer a literature-grounded mechanism or model-scope class. Supplementary Table S1 gives the framework.
When the required context was absent, a silent COMAVI result could not test the known mechanism. The isolated BRCA1 BRCT structure, for example, represents fold energetics but not phosphopeptide recognition by R1699L/Q. VHL Y98H lacks the relevant HIF-bound context, HBB E6V requires the deoxygenated polymerization state, and CALM1 N98S depends strongly on calcium-linked dynamics. These cases were treated as limitations of the supplied model rather than as negative structural evidence.
2.3 Structures and energy calculations
Complex structures were predicted with the AlphaFold 3 server [2]. Experimental structures replaced predicted models when the relevant site or interface lacked adequate confidence: 2HHB for hemoglobin, 6XI7 for KRAS-RAF1, and 1JM7 for BRCA1-BARD1. The BRCA1 tandem-BRCT monomer panel used 1JNX. All energy calculations used FoldX 5.1 [1] with default energy-function parameters. Each structure was repaired once with RepairPDB and reused across variants.
For each variant, BuildModel was run five times on the isolated subunit and on the assembled model. ΔΔG is reported as mutant minus wild type in kcal/mol, so positive values are destabilizing. The mean across replicates is the axis value, and the replicate standard deviation is retained. AnalyseComplex was then applied to each FoldX-generated mutant complex to calculate interaction-energy changes for each partner. Summary outputs use the most disruptive partner, while partner-level values remain available. Supplementary Methods S1 provides the complete commands and implementation details.
BuildModel and AnalyseComplex answer different questions. BuildModel estimates folding-energy changes for the chains and coordinates supplied to it; AnalyseComplex estimates changes in inter-chain interaction energy. Calculating both prevents an interface-localized fold signal from being mislabeled as a binding-energy change and permits combined fold-plus-binding profiles when both axes fire.
2.4 Structural confidence and usable outputs
FoldX calculates ΔΔG from the supplied coordinates; pLDDT does not numerically rescale those energies. Instead, pLDDT determines whether an energetic axis from a predicted structure can support a mechanism call or enter the priority-score energy component, and it attenuates the structural-context tier. Sites with pLDDT ≥ 70 provide primary confidence. Values from 50 to <70 are retained as lower-confidence evidence when axis-specific uncertainty checks also pass. Below 50, the predicted-structure axis is not used as mechanism evidence, and that partner does not contribute contacts, interface occupancy, or burial. For the tier, the context score is multiplied by 0.7 for best-site pLDDT 50 to <70 and by 0.4 below 50; experimental structures bypass this attenuation. Monomer-fold output is not graded in regions without an independently stable monomeric fold.
2.5 Structural-context tier and priority score
The structural-context tier describes how strongly a site is embedded in features that may make it sensitive to substitution; it contains no FoldX energy and is not itself a mechanism call. It combines substitution severity, local contacts, interface occupancy, burial, and model confidence, then maps the fixed total to Tier 1 (strongest context) through Tier 4 (weakest). Supplementary Methods S1 gives the exact weights and cutoffs.
Because interface occupancy contributes directly to the tier, we tested how much it drove the result. On the same 47-variant prioritization subset, we compared the full tier with interface status alone and with the tier recalculated without its interface bonus. Supplementary Note S5, Supplementary Figure S3, and the within-system tests report these comparisons.
The same energy and context information produces the integrated structural-disruption score (ISDS-v1), hereafter the priority score. The formula is fixed: it uses the same reference values and tier mapping for every variant set rather than fitting coefficients or standardizing within the current batch. The score ranks the strength of modeled structural-disruption evidence. It is not a pathogenicity probability, does not identify the affected structural process, and has no validated binary cutoff.
For each usable energetic calculation j, COMAVI divides |ΔΔG_j| by a fixed reference value τ_j: 2.9 kcal/mol for isolated-subunit and assembled-complex stability and 3.5 kcal/mol for binding. The largest ratio, R, represents the strongest reliable energetic signal without adding correlated isolated and assembled stability effects.
R = max_j (|ΔΔG_j| / τ_j)
COMAVI softly compresses R so that one extreme FoldX prediction cannot dominate the ranking.
E = R / (1 + R)
The tier number T is converted to a context component C, mapping Tiers 1-4 to 1.000, 0.667, 0.333, and 0.
C = (4 - T) / 3
ISDS-v1 = (E + C) / 2
The final priority score gives equal weight to energy magnitude and structural context. Only confidence-passing energy calculations enter R, while the signed values and all available axes remain visible in the mechanism profile. All four tiers contribute continuously; the separate Tier 1-2 cutoff is used only in descriptive comparator analyses and is not part of ISDS-v1. Because the score does not use the threshold that converts a continuous energy into a mechanism call, it is unchanged when that threshold changes. A value above 0.5 is not a validated positive call.
2.6 Energy thresholds and descriptive evidence states
A continuous predicted energy becomes a yes-or-no mechanism call only after an energy threshold is chosen. The threshold is the minimum absolute ΔΔG required to call that axis positive at a given operating point. We evaluated uniform thresholds of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, and 2.5 kcal/mol, plus the more conservative per-axis tSAP setting: 2.9 kcal/mol for isolated-subunit stability, 2.9 for assembled-complex stability, and 3.5 for binding [52]. The released software uses 1.0 kcal/mol as a screening-oriented default. We use 2.5 kcal/mol as the frozen benchmark reference and tSAP as the most conservative setting evaluated. None is universally optimal.
Lower thresholds call more structural effects and are useful when missing a candidate mechanism is costly. Higher thresholds make fewer calls and are useful when false mechanistic assertions are more costly. Every report should therefore show both the continuous ΔΔG and the threshold used to call it.
We also combined energy calls and tier into four descriptive states - both positive, energy only, context only, or neither - to summarize evidence patterns without redefining the physical mechanism. Definitions and analyses are in Supplementary Note S5; these states are not a validated classifier.
2.7 Benchmark outcomes and statistical analysis
For each variant, we asked whether COMAVI recovered the expected combination of applicable structural effects. The reader-facing mechanism-pattern score is the mean of a three-level partial-credit grade: 1 for a fully consistent whole-variant pattern, 0.5 for a partly correct multi-axis pattern, and 0 for an inconsistent pattern. This outcome is formally weighted mechanism consistency; it is not ordinary exact-match accuracy. Unknown or inapplicable effects were omitted. Table 1 gives the full rubric.
Table 1 | Whole-variant mechanism-pattern grading rubric.
To identify which calculations contributed, we also counted direction agreement separately for isolated-subunit stability, assembled-complex stability, and binding. Predicted-versus-measured analyses assessed rank correlation, scale compression, and the number of experimentally destabilizing effects recovered at each threshold. Continuity metrics and denominator audits are reported in Supplementary Note S7.
For structural prioritization, the positive class was the 17 variants with a modeled structural mechanism and the negative class was the 30 no-lesion variants in the 47-variant subset. ROC AUC summarizes ordering across the full list: it is the probability that a randomly selected structural-mechanism variant ranks above a randomly selected no-lesion variant. Average precision summarizes precision across the recall curve. Top-k recovery asks how many of the 17 structural mechanisms would be encountered if a researcher could test only the first 10, 20, or 25 variants. Formula comparisons, score bands, and component analyses were treated as internal sensitivity analyses because the same resource informed score development.
Variants from the same protein system share structures and curation, so the main uncertainty analyses resampled whole systems rather than treating every variant as independent. We also examined leave-one-system-out results, variant-level bootstrap intervals, label permutations, and replicate noise. Supplementary Methods S1 lists the fixed seeds and draw counts.
2.8 Software, code, and data availability
FoldX 5.1 was used for energy calculations; Python with pandas, scipy, and scikit-learn was used for scoring and statistics. Code, reference outputs, figures, and regression tests are available at https://github.com/la424/comavi. The priority score is implemented once in the shared COMAVI engine, so benchmark, CHD, generic, and live or external runs receive the same versioned fields. FoldX and large structural-input collections are not redistributed.
3. Results
3.1 COMAVI compares isolated and assembled contexts to propose experiments
COMAVI is designed to compare the same variant across structural contexts. It evaluates the isolated subunit and, when an assembly is available, the assembled complex, then separates predicted changes in subunit stability from changes in partner interaction. The priority score ranks candidates, whereas the mechanism profile retains the signed evidence needed to choose an experiment. Because the three energy changes are evaluated separately, one variant can support more than one structural hypothesis.
CFH R78G illustrates a binding-specific result: its predicted isolated-subunit change was modest (0.93 kcal/mol), whereas predicted binding disruption was 6.12 kcal/mol, consistent with an increase in the measured dissociation constant from approximately 12 μM to more than 35 μM [17]. VWF R1334Q shows the value of evaluating the assembled complex. Its isolated-subunit prediction was nearly neutral (0.01 kcal/mol), but assembled-complex stability and binding changed by 4.73 and 4.11 kcal/mol [34]. The profile therefore proposes a combined assembly-and-binding lesion rather than an isolated-fold lesion.
A silent result requires equal care. HBB E6V acts through deoxygenated hemoglobin polymerization, a state absent from the supplied model. COMAVI silence in that model cannot test the known mechanism; it redirects follow-up toward the missing assembly state. BRCA1 R1699L/Q and CALM1 N98S provide related examples involving phosphopeptide recognition and calcium-dependent dynamics.
3.2 COMAVI recovers expected mechanism patterns across diverse systems
At the 2.5 kcal/mol reference threshold, COMAVI recovered the expected combination of structural effects with a mechanism-pattern score of 41/57 = 0.719. This partial-credit result represents 41 total points across 57 graded variants, not 41 exact matches. The interaction and BRCT subsets had similar scores, so neither cohort determined the pooled result. Because the data span 14 clustered systems, 0.719 is benchmark-specific pattern agreement rather than universal accuracy.
At the output level, 65 of 85 applicable energetic calculations had the expected direction: 21/27 for isolated-subunit stability, 20/26 for stability calculated in the assembled complex, and 24/32 for binding. Figure 2 shows these axis-specific counts and the whole-variant partial-credit score. The per-axis rates identify which calculations contributed, but they do not replace the whole-variant outcome.

Evidence-restricted analyses did not change the qualitative conclusion. Among variants for which every committed axis had E1-E3 support, tier sensitivity remained 1.000 and specificity was 0.600 (n = 27); the direct-or-coupled subset gave sensitivity 1.000 and specificity 0.615 (n = 25). Because evidence class was strongly tied to whether an axis was expected to be neutral or destabilizing, these analyses were treated as sensitivity views rather than alternative primary benchmarks (Supplementary Note S2).
No single system carried the result. Resampling entire protein systems gave a 95% interval of 0.625-0.821 for the mechanism-pattern score, and leave-one-system-out values ranged from 0.704 to 0.750. Permutation, replicate-noise, and continuity audits are reported in Supplementary Note S7.
3.3 The energy threshold controls recovery and false structural calls
The energy threshold determines the balance between missed mechanisms and false structural calls. Lower thresholds identified more variants with known structural mechanisms; higher thresholds correctly left more no-lesion variants uncalled. The same tradeoff appeared in measured-energy comparisons. Of 44 experimentally destabilizing effects, 35 were detected at the 1.0 kcal/mol default, 13 at the 2.5 kcal/mol reference, and 12 at tSAP. Thus a conservative threshold can miss a real effect even when the measured destabilization is large.
FoldX preserved ordering better than magnitude. Across the broader 56-point calibration set, predicted and measured effects had Spearman ρ = 0.63, but the fitted slope was 0.43 and error became more negative as measured effects increased. Large destabilizations were therefore systematically compressed, which explains part of the sensitivity loss at high thresholds.

Together, these results support reporting the direction and rank of predicted energy, its continuous value, replicate uncertainty, and the selected threshold. They do not justify treating a FoldX ΔΔG as a measured energy or converting it to experimental units. The priority score remains continuous when the call threshold changes, so ranking and mechanism calling remain separate decisions.
3.4 Structural context and energy support practical prioritization
The structural-context tier identified all 17 variants with modeled structural mechanisms as Tier 1 or 2, but it also placed 13 of 30 no-lesion variants in those tiers. It was therefore sensitive but not specific. Interface status alone was more specific, whereas the full tier recovered two structural variants outside a detected interface. The exact comparator table and within-system tests are in Supplementary Table S7 and Supplementary Figure S3.
The tier describes whether the mutated site lies in a structurally susceptible environment. It does not identify which energetic process is affected and should not override an axis-specific energy prediction. Its components therefore remain visible, and its fixed cut-points require prospective testing.
We next asked whether combining energy magnitude and structural context moved variants with modeled structural lesions toward the top of a candidate list. This analysis used the overlapping set of 47 interaction variants with a committed structural endpoint: 17 structural-mechanism variants and 30 variants curated to lack a lesion on the modeled axes. The fixed priority score is defined in Methods.
The priority score placed 16 of 17 structural-mechanism variants in the top 20 and all 17 in the top 25. Across the complete 47-variant ranking, structural-prioritization ROC AUC was 0.943 and average precision was 0.818. These are internal ranking results, not external performance estimates, pathogenicity estimates, or calibrated probabilities.
Figure 6 shows the score distributions and recovery within a limited experimental budget. The soft-saturation transformation, component ROC curves, score bands, leave-one-system-out influence, and alternative fixed formulas are reported in Supplementary Note S5 and Supplementary Figure S4.

3.5 COMAVI complements pathogenicity prediction and directs follow-up experiments
Pathogenicity prediction, structural prioritization, and mechanism interpretation answer different questions. On the common clinically labelled set of 47 variants, AlphaMissense was the stronger pathogenicity ranker (ROC AUC 0.903); the priority score had pathogenicity ROC AUC 0.851, its energy component 0.836, and its context component 0.769. These pathogenicity AUCs should not be compared directly with the priority score's structural-prioritization ROC AUC of 0.943 because the positive definition and task differ. AlphaMissense asks whether a variant may be pathogenic; the priority score asks how strongly the available structure supports modeled disruption; the mechanism profile identifies the proposed structural process and experiment.
Discordant cases show why all three views are useful. HBB E6V can be pathogenic despite a low structural score because its polymerization mechanism is absent from the supplied state. CFH R78G and VWF R1334Q show the converse: multimer-aware profiles identify binding-specific or combined assembly-and-binding hypotheses that a pathogenicity score cannot name. PIK3CA H1047R and TNNI3 R162W occupy strong structural contexts without a thresholded energetic lesion, supporting follow-up without inventing a mechanism.

Orange points are pathogenic or pathogenic gain-of-function variants; blue points are benign; the gray band marks the AlphaMissense ambiguous range. COMAVI scores remain structural descriptors, and the mechanism profile provides the process-specific interpretation.
Silent structural results occurred in both pathogenic and benign variants in this selected benchmark (Supplementary Note S6). The transferable conclusion is therefore asymmetric: a positive structural call supplies a testable hypothesis, whereas a silent call provides little evidence against pathogenicity.
A practical COMAVI workflow has two steps. First, use the priority score to decide which variants deserve structural follow-up. Second, inspect the signed isolated-subunit, assembled-complex, and binding predictions, together with uncertainty, threshold, tier components, and model limitations, to choose the experiment. A high score without a thresholded energy call remains context-based prioritization rather than a named lesion.
4. Discussion
4.1 Two linked outputs answer two practical questions
COMAVI helps answer two practical questions: which variants deserve structural follow-up, and which experiment should test the predicted effect. At the 2.5 kcal/mol reference threshold, the mechanism profile recovered the expected structural pattern with a partial-credit score of 41/57 = 0.719. In the overlapping 47-variant prioritization subset, the priority score placed 16 of 17 modeled structural mechanisms within the top 20 candidates. These results support mechanism-directed triage and prioritization within the present literature-curated resource; they do not establish universal accuracy, clinical validity, or external generalization.
COMAVI's main scientific advantage is that it evaluates the same substitution in an isolated subunit and in the assembled complex. This comparison can distinguish a predicted folding problem from a predicted partner-binding problem or identify both. CFH R78G and VWF R1334Q show how that decomposition maps to affinity or assembly experiments, whereas HBB E6V shows why a missing structural state must limit interpretation.
The priority score provides a transparent way to allocate limited experimental attention, but it cannot identify the affected process. The mechanism profile remains necessary for interpretation. The tier describes site context; it does not select a FoldX axis or override an axis-specific energy result. Prospective testing on new protein systems is therefore essential.
4.2 How to use COMAVI: rank, interpret, and test
Threshold choice should follow the cost of error. The 1.0 kcal/mol default is screening-oriented; 1.5-2.0 provides intermediate stringency; 2.5 is the frozen benchmark reference; and tSAP is the most conservative setting evaluated. Lower thresholds are preferable when missing a candidate mechanism is more costly, whereas higher thresholds are preferable when false structural assertions are more costly.
Every call should retain its continuous energy and threshold because near-boundary variants lose information when reduced to a binary label. A silent result must not be translated into benign, nonfunctional, or unworthy of study. It means only that the supplied static model did not show a structural lesion at the selected threshold.
COMAVI should be used alongside pathogenicity predictors, population evidence, functional assays, and clinical interpretation frameworks such as Franklin [5]. The practical sequence is simple: use the priority score to allocate attention, then use the signed mechanism profile and model limitations to design the experiment. Pathogenicity evidence remains a separate question.
4.3 Limitations and validation priorities
FoldX predictions preserve direction and rank better than absolute magnitude. Large destabilizations are compressed, so stringent thresholds can miss real effects and the priority score cannot be interpreted in experimental energy units. The score's compression of extreme predictions limits their influence but does not calibrate FoldX.
The benchmark is small and clustered: 57 mechanism-evaluable variants span 14 systems, and variants within a system share structures and curation. Whole-system resampling addresses that dependence but cannot replace prospective validation. The priority-score formula and its initial evaluation used the same literature-curated resource, so the reported structural-prioritization ROC AUC and average precision are internal results that require testing without retuning. Evidence quality is also uneven: isolated-subunit stability and binding have direct energy anchors, whereas the present benchmark contains no direct measured energy comparator for stability calculated in the assembled complex.
Finally, COMAVI evaluates the supplied static coordinates. It cannot capture every catalytic, dynamic, aggregative, regulatory, post-translational, ligand-dependent, or state-dependent mechanism. Model limitations must therefore be assessed before a silent result is interpreted. Versioned literature curation remains important, and detailed evidence updates are documented in the supplement and repository.
The highest-value next tests are external panels that separate folding from binding and include both disruptive and retained-function variants. Additional VWF and CFH variants can strengthen validation of fold-neutral binding lesions; SOD1, transthyretin, and dense PDZ, SH3, and GB1 landscapes can test coupled tertiary, quaternary, interface, and allosteric effects [49-51]. The priority-score formula is now frozen and should be evaluated on these systems without retuning. The current priority analysis is restricted to interaction variants because its structural-context tier was defined and evaluated in that setting. Extending a comparable tier and priority score to monomer-only systems would require a prespecified formula and prospective validation rather than reusing the current cut-points by assumption.
4.4 Conclusion
COMAVI converts a list of missense variants into two linked outputs: a fixed priority for structural follow-up and a mechanism profile that identifies the proposed structural process and experimental test. In this benchmark, it recovered useful mechanism patterns and concentrated modeled structural mechanisms near the top of the ranking. Its value lies in directing experiments, not replacing pathogenicity prediction. Responsible use requires the priority score, signed profile, selected threshold, structural context, and model limitations to be interpreted together.

Supplementary Methods S1 - Implementation details and statistical conventions
Each structural context was repaired once and cached. Mutations were specified in FoldX notation. BuildModel was run five times on both isolated and assembled coordinates, and AnalyseComplex was applied pairwise to each resulting mutant complex. Beyond the command, input, replicate count, partner chains, and output directory, no FoldX energy-function parameter was overridden.
foldx --command=RepairPDB --pdb=<structure> --output-dir=<workdir>
foldx --command=BuildModel --pdb=<repaired> --mutant-file=individual_list.txt --numberOfRuns=5 --output-dir=<workdir>
foldx --command=AnalyseComplex --pdb=<mutant_complex> --analyseComplexChains=<chainA>,<chainB> --output-dir=<workdir>
The mutant-minus-wild-type ΔΔG was read from FoldX outputs. Wild-type interaction energy was computed once per partner pair, and mutant interaction energy was computed on each of the five BuildModel replicates. Partner-level binding ΔΔG is the mean of IE(mutant) - IE(wild type); replicate standard deviation is retained.
Structural-context tier implementation. The raw score is Grantham substitution severity multiplied by the sum of monomer contacts and the maximum confidence-passing inter-chain contact count, plus an interface bonus (1.5 for one partner; 2.0 for at least two) and a burial bonus (1.0 for partial burial; 2.0 for a buried core position). The result is multiplied by 1.0 for pLDDT at least 70, 0.7 for pLDDT 50-70, and 0.4 below 50; experimental structures bypass this attenuation. Fixed cutoffs assign Tier 1 at s at least 5.0, Tier 2 at 3.0 to less than 5.0, Tier 3 at 1.5 to less than 3.0, and Tier 4 below 1.5.
Table S1 | Model-scope and mechanism-eligibility framework.
Systems, not variants, are the primary resampling unit because variants within one system share a structure, partner set, and curation process. The public mechanism-localization stress suite uses fixed seeds with 2,000 ground-truth block permutations (seed 20260729), 500 replicate-noise draws (seed 11), 2,000 variant-bootstrap draws (seed 7), and 2,000 whole-system bootstrap draws (seed 8). The priority-score analysis uses 10,000 whole-system bootstrap draws (seed 20260821) for ROC AUC, average precision, and paired AUC differences. The paired threshold-tradeoff analysis uses 10,000 whole-system draws (seed 42); the tier-pathogenicity gradient uses 5,000 whole-system draws (seed 11); and the convergence-specificity gain uses 200,000 whole-system draws (seed 0). Tier associations also use Fisher exact tests, exact within-system permutation, and leave-one-system-out diagnostics. Where families of related 2 × 2 tests are reported across thresholds, both raw and Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted p values are retained.
The priority score is calculated after energetic confidence and structural-context tier assessment and before threshold-specific mechanism classification. The shared engine writes nine versioned fields: ISDS-v1, energy and context components, the uncapped energy ratio, dominant axis and partner, dominant signed ΔΔG, an availability flag, and the formula version. The locked analysis plan, formula constants, missing-data rules, random seed, bootstrap count, and sensitivity transformations are distributed as YAML and JSON with checksums. Unit tests cover reference values, tier mapping, batch invariance, monotonicity, stabilization, partner selection, confidence gating, and missing inputs.
Supplementary Note S2 - Evidence provenance and denominator sensitivity
The evidence ledger contains 109 committed energetic-axis expectations after the A636P correction. E1-E3 expectations are supported by energetic, functional, or qualitative experiments. E4 denotes population-supported neutral expectations. E5 denotes an inferred axis state used when the literature supported a mechanistic or retained-function interpretation but no axis-specific assay was available; inference was not assigned from missing data alone. E4 and E5 commitments are heavily enriched for neutral expectations: all 9 E4 axes and 23/24 E5 axes are neutral. The single E5 destabilizing expectation is the complex-context stability lesion assigned to MLH1 L749P. Complex-context stability is the least independently grounded axis, with 13/29 expectations inferred.

Supplementary Note S4 - Operating-point and calibration diagnostics
The apparent pooled optimum depends on class mixture. The benchmark contains 25 variants with committed structural mechanisms and 32 benchmark negatives (structural fraction 0.439). Reweighting the classes moved the pooled optimum: 2.5 remained optimal only while the structural fraction was below 0.646; the optimum moved to 1.5 above 0.646 and to 1.0 above 0.796. This analysis supports reporting a threshold menu rather than a universal optimum.
In 10,000 paired whole-system resamples (seed 42), mechanism recovery decreased from 0.760 (95% CI 0.639-0.862) at 1.0 to 0.540 (0.400-0.680) at tSAP, whereas correct rejection increased from 0.438 (0.273-0.633) to 0.844 (0.690-1.000). The joint probability that recovery fell and rejection rose was 0.9996. The direction of the curves is forced by thresholding, however; resampling quantifies the size of the exchange, not its existence.
The mechanism-attribution menu also answers a different question from the 3 kcal/mol fold cutoff used for cellular-stability or degradation interpretation in MAVISp [36] and related MLH1 studies [37,38]. A predicted fold change below that cellular-abundance cutoff can still identify the correct structural axis for follow-up. We therefore retain the 3 kcal/mol comparison as endpoint context, not as a basis for selecting a COMAVI operating point.

Six catalytic or signaling variants - KRAS G12D, G12V, and Q61H; PIK3CA H1047R; and SMAD4 I500T and I500V - had no destabilizing energetic call at 2.5 and were mechanism-consistent with a no-modeled-static-lesion expectation. H1047R and I500T nonetheless occupied Tier 2 contexts, indicating structurally important sites without assigning an energetic lesion. Among the two PIK3CA helical-domain variants at the PIK3CA-PIK3R1 inhibitory interface, E542K was partially resolved as an interface-associated fold signal, whereas E545K was a transparent wrong-direction binding call.
The CFH and VWF cases exercise the complementary fold-neutral interface regime. CFH R78G is binding-dominant (monomer 0.93, binding 6.12 kcal/mol; measured K_D approximately 12 μM to >35 μM). VWF R1334Q has an approximately neutral monomer output (0.01) but complex-context and binding outputs of 4.73 and 4.11 kcal/mol. With only two disruptive examples, these cases demonstrate intended behavior rather than a general detection advantage.
Supplementary Note S7 - Robustness, uncertainty, and metric reconciliation
The versioned per-variant mechanism grades and output-level agreement fields are the authoritative source records. In the manuscript, structural agreement is described as a four-output continuity aggregate and is decomposed into the three energetic axes plus tier. The primary convention excludes the four ungraded scope or commitment cases from both whole-variant mechanism scoring and the continuity denominator, giving 99/132 = 0.750 at 2.5 and 97/132 = 0.735 at tSAP. The all-row denominator audit is 99/133 = 0.744 at 2.5. Naive recounts based only on raw ΔΔG sign or per-partner values do not reproduce the metric because the shipped scorer applies direction, operating-point, confidence, and interval gates.
Restricting the primary graded set to crystal or confident site models removes the seven partial-site variants. A636P remains in this sensitivity because it is a confident-site case, so its two newly committed neutral fold expectations still count as prediction disagreements. The confidence restriction therefore removes lower-confidence observations without turning the remaining cohort into a hidden high-performing subset; it should be read as a robustness view, not as an alternative benchmark definition.
Table S12 | Mechanism-pattern and four-output robustness analyses at the 2.5 kcal/mol reference.
In the replicate-noise test, every ΔΔG was perturbed by Gaussian noise at the standard error of its five-run mean, mechanism labels were re-derived from the perturbed values, and variants were regraded across 500 draws (seed 11). Fifty-two of 61 variants never changed mechanism label, and 30% of draws reproduced all 61 labels. The nine marginal variants lie near an operating point and should be interpreted as threshold-sensitive.
A silent implementation error was identified and corrected during development. Grading perturbed energies against stored mechanism labels makes weighted mechanism consistency mathematically incapable of moving and produces an apparent standard deviation of zero. The reported 0.013 standard deviation comes from the corrected implementation, which re-derives the mechanism label after perturbation. Both stages remain in the repository with fixed seeds for auditability.

The hemoglobin series provides a high-quality within-system anchor but does not test generalization across unrelated interfaces. Measured binding ΔΔG was calculated as RT ln(K_D,mut/K_D,wt).
